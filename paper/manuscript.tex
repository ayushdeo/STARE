\begin{abstract}
Region-based spatial transcriptomics measures only part of a tissue section, and several recent methods choose
each next region from the expression already measured. We asked whether this feedback helps, for two objectives
that reward different choices: reconstructing expression across the section and capturing regions of high
molecular heterogeneity. In a retrospective simulator built from 22 human HEST-1k slides (18 studies, four
tissues), we held the image encoder and imputer fixed and compared value-conditioned policies with non-adaptive
placement in three analysis stages, each registered before its experiments, with inference that resamples whole
studies. For reconstruction, a policy designed to target the imputer's prediction error did worse than a
non-adaptive rule chosen on held-out studies at every budget from 0.5\% to 20\% of regions. When a fixed coverage
design spent half or more of the budget and feedback chose the rest, the deficit largely disappeared, but no
hybrid improved on the coverage design after correction: from 2\% upward, all were within 0.007 PCC and 0.006
recall of it. The measured values were informative, since the same policies did worse when shown permuted
values, but that information mostly offset the cost of leaving the coverage design. For detection, informative
feedback raised recall over permuted feedback by up to 0.063 without surviving correction, an advantage over a
fixed MI-greedy design did not hold against the non-adaptive rule chosen for detection, and a non-adaptive
diversity sampler performed about as well. A semi-synthetic control did not establish that the protocol could
detect a reconstruction gain. Within this retrospective setting, with computationally defined regions of interest
and spot-sized units, the tested feedback policies offered no supported advantage over well-chosen non-adaptive
placement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Whole-transcriptome spatial assays are expensive, and region-of-interest platforms such as GeoMx
DSP~\citep{merritt2020geomx} profile only part of a section. The rest can be predicted afterwards, usually from
co-registered hematoxylin and eosin (H\&E) images~\citep{jaume2024hest,xie2023bleep}. Which regions are
measured therefore shapes two outcomes: how well the unmeasured tissue can be reconstructed, and whether
biologically distinct regions are measured at all.

Recent methods choose regions sequentially. SOFisher~\citep{li2026sofisher} trains a reinforcement-learning
policy that moves fields of view toward regions enriched for a target signal, using the expression measured so
far. SCR$^2$-ST~\citep{zhu2025scr2st} also uses reinforcement learning, choosing sampling locations for
expression prediction with a reward dominated by coverage of a single-cell reference. S2-omics~\citep{yuan2025s2omics}
instead selects regions from histology before any measurement, aiming to capture molecular heterogeneity, and its
preprint argues that waiting for measured expression between choices is impractical. These methods serve
different goals. SOFisher and S2-omics try to capture particular regions, while reconstruction tries to predict
expression everywhere.

The two goals lead to different expectations about feedback. For reconstruction, Gaussian-process sensor
placement suggests that feedback should matter little: with a fixed kernel, posterior variance depends on where
measurements are taken and not on the values observed, so the best adaptive and non-adaptive designs
coincide~\citep{krause2008near}. Detection has no comparable argument, because the targets are defined by the
values themselves. Evaluating either goal also requires a fair baseline. A placement rule that could be chosen
before profiling a new slide is the relevant competitor, not the best rule for that slide picked in hindsight.

We therefore asked three questions with the image encoder and imputer held fixed:
\textbf{Q1}, does conditioning region choice on measured expression improve whole-section reconstruction over a
feasible non-adaptive rule?
\textbf{Q2}, does informative feedback improve the capture of molecularly heterogeneous regions?
A policy that abandons coverage could fail for reasons unrelated to feedback, so for both objectives we also
asked whether feedback adds to a strong coverage design when it chooses only the remainder of the budget.
\textbf{Q3}, an exploratory stress test, asked whether a detection advantage persists when measurements arrive
in batches, as they do on real platforms.
To separate the contribution of feedback from that of a selection rule, we compared each value-conditioned policy
with the same policy given uninformative values. To learn whether a null result could have been otherwise, we ran
synthetic and semi-synthetic positive controls.

None of the tested value-conditioned policies improved reconstruction, and feedback layered on a coverage design
changed neither objective by a detectable amount, although the measured values were informative. The
semi-synthetic control did not establish that the protocol would detect a reconstruction gain if one existed.
For detection, feedback produced positive but uncertain gains whose size depended on the comparator, and a
non-adaptive diversity design matched them. The study contributes an evaluation rather than a method: a
reproducible protocol on 22 human slides from 18 studies, analyses registered before each experimental stage, and
the finding that the tested feedback policies offered no supported advantage over well-chosen non-adaptive
placement for either objective.

\section{Related work}

Expression has been predicted from H\&E with contrastive retrieval~\citep{xie2023bleep}, flow matching over
spatial neighbourhoods~\citep{huang2025stflow}, diffusion~\citep{zhu2025stem}, and autoregressive
generation~\citep{genar2025}, and benchmarked on HEST-1k~\citep{jaume2024hest}. We hold the predictor fixed and
vary only the regions it receives. For deciding what to measure, gene-panel selectors fix a set of
genes~\citep{covert2023persist,reconst2026}, and region selectors range from one-shot image
rankers~\citep{spatialfinder2026} and histology-based heterogeneity selection~\citep{yuan2025s2omics} to
sequential reinforcement-learning samplers~\citep{li2026sofisher,zhu2025scr2st}. Active feature acquisition
casts measurement as a sequential policy~\citep{yin2020afa}. Variance- and mutual-information-greedy placement
are near-optimal for reconstructing a Gaussian-process field~\citep{krause2008near} and serve here as non-adaptive
baselines.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Slides and acquisition unit}
We used 22 human HEST-1k slides~\citep{jaume2024hest}: prostate (6), kidney (4), lung (6), and colon (6), of which
7 are Visium HD and 15 standard Visium (Appendix~\ref{app:slides}). Selection rules were fixed before any result
on these slides: human tissue, whole-transcriptome assay, at least 1{,}000 spots under tissue, a source image of
at most 0.6\,$\mu$m/px so that image inputs are comparable, one slide per specimen, and at most two slides per
study to limit dependence. The slides come from 18 HEST studies, which we treat as the independent units.

A region is one native spot. Visium spots are 55\,$\mu$m wide on a 100\,$\mu$m pitch; the Visium HD slides, as
distributed by HEST, have a median spacing of 128\,$\mu$m on our slides. We used the spot because HEST provides
expression and an image patch at that unit, and because a fixed square grid would pool different numbers of
spots on slides with different pixel sizes. Slides contain 1{,}766 to 9{,}080 regions. The image input is the
HEST 224\,px patch at 0.5\,$\mu$m/px centred on each spot, embedded with frozen UNI2-h~\citep{chen2024uni}.
Expression is log1p-transformed, and genes detected in fewer than 10\% of regions are removed.

\subsection{Objectives}
A policy reveals regions up to a budget, given as a fraction of the slide's regions (0.5\% to 20\%), and each
revealed region exposes its measured expression. For \emph{reconstruction} (Q1), a fixed imputer predicts the
unprofiled regions and we score the per-gene Pearson correlation over those regions, averaged over the 50 most
variable genes (PCC), the HEST-Benchmark target~\citep{jaume2024hest}. For \emph{detection} (Q2, Q3), regions of
interest (ROIs) are the 20\% of regions with the highest local molecular heterogeneity, the variance of
top-50-gene expression across a region's 8 nearest spatial neighbours, following the heterogeneity objective of
S2-omics~\citep{yuan2025s2omics}. We score recall, the fraction of ROIs that were profiled. These ROIs are
computational; they are not annotated regions of biological interest. Gene sets and ROIs come from ground truth and
are used only for scoring.

\subsection{Imputer}
The imputer (graphflow) maps image principal components through 512 random Fourier features, fits a ridge
regression to the profiled regions, and runs 20 steps of message passing on a fixed 8-nearest-neighbour graph in
image and spatial features, holding profiled regions at their measured values. One fixed imputer serves every
policy, so differences between policies reflect selection alone. Its predictions depend on the measured values,
but its uncertainty, one minus the graph influence that reaches a region from profiled nodes, depends only on which
regions have been profiled. A $k$-nearest-neighbour and a regression-kriging imputer were run on an earlier slide
set (Appendix~\ref{app:imputers}).

\subsection{Policies}
\emph{Non-adaptive rules}: random, farthest-point in space (grid), $k$-means on image embeddings with medoids, a
reimplementation of a one-shot image ranker~\citep{spatialfinder2026}, and variance-greedy and
mutual-information (MI)-greedy placement with an RBF kernel on image and coordinate
features~\citep{krause2008near}. \emph{Sequential fixed designs}: error-reduction picks the region whose 15 nearest
unprofiled neighbours (in the imputer's feature space) have the largest summed uncertainty, and
maximum-uncertainty picks the most uncertain region. Under graphflow neither depends on measured values (for error-reduction we confirmed
on a test slide that permuted values yield identical selections), so they are fixed designs computed one region at a
time. \emph{Value-conditioned policies}: the \emph{residual} policy keeps the error-reduction form
but replaces the uncertainty with an estimate of the imputer's actual error. It takes the leave-one-out residual
of the image ridge model at each profiled region, averages it over each unprofiled region's 10 nearest profiled
neighbours, and multiplies by one minus the graph influence, so it targets reconstruction error through the
measured values. The \emph{local-variance} policy scores a candidate by the kernel-weighted variance of measured
expression around it plus 0.5 times its distance to the nearest profiled region; it seeks heterogeneous areas and
is the detection policy. \emph{Competitor objectives}: a non-adaptive diversity sampler (farthest-point in image
and space, after the coverage-dominated reward of SCR$^2$-ST~\citep{zhu2025scr2st}) and an adaptive ROI-seeking
sampler after SOFisher~\citep{li2026sofisher}. These reimplement stated objectives and are not the authors'
trained models.

To isolate feedback, each value-conditioned policy was also run with \emph{permuted} feedback, in which it sees
the expression of a randomly chosen other region whenever it profiles a region, and with \emph{constant} feedback,
in which every region appears to have the slide's mean expression. The selection rule is unchanged; only the
information in the values is removed. Under permuted feedback, for example, the residual policy's leave-one-out
ridge model is fitted to the permuted values it is shown. The imputer and the scores always use the true values.
Stage 2 used one permutation (seed 2021); stage 3 ran two per configuration (seeds 1 and 2) and averaged them, and
repeated the stage-2 ablations with further seeds (1--4 for local-variance, 1--2 for the residual policy).

\emph{Coverage plus feedback.} A hybrid takes its first $\lceil fn\rceil$ regions, for a budget of $n$ regions and
$f\in\{0.5,0.8\}$, from a fixed coverage rule, and the rest from a feedback policy that conditions on everything
measured so far: the residual policy for reconstruction and local-variance for detection. The coverage rules
select in a fixed order, so the first phase is exactly the prefix of the rule's own full-budget selection, which we
verified for all four rules at both fractions and two budgets. A hybrid and its coverage rule therefore differ only
in how the last $1-f$ of the budget is spent.

\subsection{Comparators}
The primary comparator is a \emph{feasible} rule chosen without the target slide. For each held-out study, we
select the non-adaptive rule (grid, $k$-means, variance-greedy, MI-greedy, or one-shot) with the best mean score on
the other 17 studies at that budget and objective, and apply it to the held-out slides. It selected $k$-means for
reconstruction at 0.5--1\% and MI-greedy at 2--20\% (with $k$-means for 4 of 18 studies at 2\%), and for detection
variance-greedy for most studies at 2--10\% and the one-shot ranker at 20\% (Appendix~\ref{app:comparators}). The per-slide best rule,
which uses each slide's outcome, is kept only as a hindsight benchmark. Stage 3 compared each hybrid with one rule
fixed for all slides: the rule the feasible procedure chose for most held-out studies at that budget and
objective. These rules were chosen on stage-2 results from the same slides, which favours the baseline. The
stage-1 batched experiments (Q3) used the per-slide best of $k$-means, grid, and MI-greedy; stage 3 repeated them
against the fixed detection rule.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
Each margin is a per-slide difference between two policies. We report its mean, a 95\% percentile bootstrap CI
that resamples whole studies (5{,}000 resamples), and an exact two-sided sign-flip test that flips whole studies
(all $2^{18}$ sign patterns). The CIs are not adjusted for multiplicity; $p_H$ denotes Holm-adjusted
$p$-values~\citep{holm1979simple}. We also report the minimum detectable effect: the smallest true mean margin
that the test would detect with 80\% power, estimated by resampling studies around the observed dispersion. For a
registered family of $m$ margins we compute it at $\alpha=0.05/m$ (MDE$_H$), a conservative bound for the Holm
decision rule; values at an unadjusted $\alpha=0.05$ are descriptive only.

The sign-flip test treats the study margins as exchangeable. This holds for a fixed comparator but not exactly
for the feasible one, which is chosen for each study from the other 17, so the 18 margins share the selection
step. We therefore checked the stage-2 inference post hoc with a bootstrap that repeats the selection inside every
resample and with a single fixed comparator (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}). Stage 3 used fixed comparators only.

The analyses were registered in the repository in three stages, each before its experiments were run and after the
previous stage's results were known. Stage 1 compared policies with per-slide best rules in a family of 24 margins
(Appendix~\ref{app:stage1}); the first batched and contiguous experiments for Q3 come from this stage. Stage 2
introduced the feasible comparator, the residual policy, and the feedback ablations, with a primary family of 18
margins: residual minus feasible for reconstruction at six budgets (Q1), and for detection at four budgets,
local-variance minus its permuted-feedback version, minus the feasible rule, and minus the diversity sampler (Q2).
Stage 3 added the hybrids. Its primary family of 20 margins was each hybrid minus the same coverage rule run for the
whole budget, for both fractions, at six budgets for reconstruction and four for detection. Its secondary family of
24 compared each hybrid with its permuted-feedback version and, for local-variance in rounds of 5 at 10 and 20\%,
compared the policy with the fixed detection rule and with permuted feedback. In every stage the decision rule was to claim a benefit only for a
positive margin with $p_H<0.05$ and to report any other outcome as not established. Results are labelled
confirmatory (registered primary), secondary (registered, Holm within the secondary family), descriptive, or post
hoc.

\subsection{Positive controls}
A null result is informative only if the protocol could have registered an effect. We used two controls. In
synthetic slides of $20\times20$ regions with image-unpredictable, spatially clustered structure (8 slides per
setting, 10\% budget, $k$-NN imputer), we varied the correlation length of the hidden structure and the amplitude
$\alpha$ with which ROIs appear in measured expression (descriptive). On the 22 real slides we added 20 genes
carrying three hotspots (radius 5\% of the slide diagonal) that the image cannot predict, scaled so that each
gene's standard deviation is $\alpha$ times the median of the slide's top-50 genes, with $\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}$.
Before running it, we set the criterion that the control succeeds if the residual policy beats the feasible rule
with a CI excluding zero at $\alpha=2$.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Positive controls}
The synthetic controls showed that the simulator can register an effect of feedback on detection. The
local-variance policy's recall margin over coverage rose with ROI amplitude, from $-0.045$ at $\alpha=0$ to
$+0.052$ (95\% CI $+0.033$ to $+0.071$) at $\alpha=4$ and $+0.101$ ($+0.065$ to $+0.135$) at $\alpha=8$, positive
on all 8 slides at both amplitudes (Figure~\ref{fig:controls}b). Reconstruction behaved differently. A
non-deployable ordering by true local difficulty stayed below coverage at every correlation length, by $0.105$ and
$0.066$ PCC at the two shortest lengths and $0.013$ to $0.020$ at the longer ones (Figure~\ref{fig:controls}a).

The semi-synthetic control on real slides did not meet its criterion. At $\alpha=1$ and $2$ all 20 injected genes
entered the evaluation set on every slide, yet the residual policy remained below the feasible rule:
$-0.021$ ($-0.030$ to $-0.012$) at 5\% and $-0.011$ ($-0.019$ to $-0.004$) at 10\% for $\alpha=2$, close to its
deficits without injection ($-0.015$ and $-0.007$; Figure~\ref{fig:main}c, Appendix~\ref{app:spike}). Feedback still
helped relative to permuted values, by $+0.001$ to $+0.013$. The protocol can therefore register a large detection
effect, but we could not show that it would register a reconstruction gain, and the Q1 results below should be read
as statements about the tested policies.

\subsection{Q1: Reconstruction}
No value-conditioned policy outperformed the feasible rule (Tables~\ref{tab:q1abs} and~\ref{tab:q1},
Figure~\ref{fig:main}a). The residual policy was below it at every budget, by $0.092$ PCC at 0.5\%, $0.057$ at 1\%,
$0.025$ at 2\%, $0.016$ at 5\%, $0.007$ at 10\%, and $0.002$ at 20\%. The deficit was significant at 0.5, 1, 5, and
10\% ($p_H\le0.010$). Under the registered rule, Q1 has no benefit to report. The local-variance policy, which seeks
heterogeneous areas, was further below, by $0.119$ to $0.163$ PCC (secondary, $p_H\le0.001$). Treating the
comparator's selection as part of the inference does not change this: a bootstrap that repeats the
leave-one-study-out choice inside each resample gave intervals below zero at every budget (post hoc;
Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}).

The feedback ablation shows that measured values did carry information for reconstruction. Given informative
rather than permuted values, the residual policy improved by $+0.031$, $+0.039$, and $+0.030$ PCC at 0.5--2\%
(secondary; $p_H$ 0.056, 0.002, and 0.014), with no difference at 10--20\%. Feedback thus moved the residual policy
toward the feasible rule without closing the gap. The policy's error-reduction structure is the likely reason. The
sequential fixed design with the same structure but value-independent uncertainty trailed the feasible rule by
similar amounts at low budgets ($-0.107$, $-0.067$, and $-0.024$ at 0.5--2\%; post hoc), and both converged to it as
the budget grew. At 20\% the maximum-uncertainty design was $+0.003$ PCC above the feasible rule (95\% CI $+0.002$ to
$+0.004$, $p=0.0007$, post hoc), a small advantage of a fixed design that involves no feedback.

The hindsight benchmark changed the size of these deficits but not their direction. The per-slide best rule exceeded
the feasible rule by $0.016$ to $0.021$ PCC at budgets of 2\% or less and by at most $0.002$ above that
(Appendix~\ref{app:comparators}). Section~\ref{sec:hybrid} asks whether the same feedback helps when it does not
replace coverage.

\subsection{Q2: Detection}
Informative feedback raised the local-variance policy's recall over permuted feedback by $+0.005$, $+0.017$,
$+0.029$, and $+0.063$ at 2, 5, 10, and 20\% (Table~\ref{tab:q2}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}b). The unadjusted CIs exclude
zero at 5--20\%, but none survived adjustment ($p_H\ge0.29$). Each estimate also lies below the family-wise MDE
at its budget (0.015, 0.035, 0.066, and 0.112), so the experiment could miss effects of this size. The margin over
the feasible rule was smaller ($+0.006$ to $+0.026$, $p_H\ge0.25$), and the margin over the diversity sampler was
close to zero ($+0.001$ to $+0.012$, $p_H=1.000$).

How large the detection advantage looks depends on the comparator. Against MI-greedy fixed for every slide, for
which the sign-flip test is valid as specified, the local-variance policy gained $+0.043$ and $+0.078$ recall at 10
and 20\% ($p_H=0.043$ for both, Holm over the 18 analogous margins; post hoc). MI-greedy, however, is a design for
reconstruction and the weakest detection rule in the pool at 5--20\% (0.200 recall at 20\%, against 0.247 for
variance-greedy and 0.252 for the one-shot ranker), and the bootstrap that repeats the feasible selection gave
intervals that include zero at every budget (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}). Feedback-driven detection thus beat
a coverage design aimed at the other objective, but not the non-adaptive rule chosen for detection.

The absolute recalls show why the feedback margin exceeds the margin over the feasible rule
(Table~\ref{tab:q2abs}). Without informative feedback the local-variance policy fell below the feasible rule
(0.215 with permuted and 0.204 with constant values, against 0.252 at 20\%), and informative feedback lifted it to
0.278. Part of the feedback effect therefore recovers ground the selection rule loses, rather than adding to what a
good non-adaptive design achieves. The diversity sampler reached 0.266 without any feedback. Against the
exploration-only variant, feedback was significant at 20\% ($+0.074$, $p_H=0.038$, secondary). The ROI-seeking
sampler did not improve recall over coverage in stage 1 (Appendix~\ref{app:stage1}). Because that port was not
the trained SOFisher, we also ran SOFisher natively on its own mouse cortex data. Its evaluation reward for captured
target regions rose from a median of $-238$ early in training to $+147$ late (descriptive;
Appendix~\ref{app:sofisher}). The port's result on our slides is therefore not a test of SOFisher. SCR$^2$-ST could
not be run natively because its processed training data are not public.

\subsection{Feedback on top of a coverage design}
\label{sec:hybrid}
The residual and local-variance policies replace coverage with feedback from the first region. The hybrids test
the more favourable arrangement, in which a strong fixed design spends most of the budget and feedback chooses only
the remainder (Table~\ref{tab:s3}, Figure~\ref{fig:s3}). For reconstruction, the hybrids recovered most of the
residual policy's deficit, yet none improved on the coverage rule. With coverage for half the budget the hybrid
was still below $k$-means at 0.5\%, by $0.036$ PCC ($p_H=0.006$), and from 2\% upward every hybrid was within
$0.007$ PCC of MI-greedy with no margin significant. For detection, every hybrid margin lay between $-0.003$ and
$+0.006$ recall ($p_H\ge0.76$). These nulls are tight at most budgets. At 2--10\% the family-wise MDEs were 0.004 to
0.017 PCC and 0.004 to 0.026 recall, so a hybrid gain larger than about 0.02 PCC or 0.03 recall would probably have
been detected there. At 20\% the detection bound widens to 0.035--0.068.

The feedback was not empty. Shown permuted rather than real values, the hybrids did worse at 2\%, by $0.016$ and
$0.007$ PCC for the two reconstruction hybrids and $0.007$ recall for the half-coverage detection hybrid
(secondary, $p_H\le0.027$). For that detection hybrid the difference grew to $0.014$--$0.035$ at 5--20\% without
surviving correction, while for reconstruction at 10--20\% the permuted versions were marginally ahead, by at most
$0.003$ PCC ($p_H\ge0.12$; Appendix~\ref{app:stage3}). The absolute values show where the information went
(Table~\ref{tab:s3abs}). With permuted values, the feedback phase lost ground relative to continuing the coverage
rule, for example from 0.127 to 0.110 recall at 10\%, and real values brought the hybrid back to about the level of
the rule (0.133). Feedback thus mostly repaired the cost of leaving the coverage design rather than adding to it.
In detection, the local-variance policy run alone (0.137 recall at 10\%, 0.278 at 20\%) was ahead of both hybrids
at every budget (descriptive), so giving part of the budget to the detection rule did not help it.

\subsection{Q3: Batched and contiguous acquisition (exploratory)}
Real platforms rarely let a policy see each measurement before choosing the next region. We treat Q3 as an
exploratory stress test, because Q2 established no advantage for batching to erode. In stage 1, regions were
revealed at 10\% budget in rounds of 1, 5, or 25, or in one round, with the policy using only measurements
available at the start of each round, and separately in contiguous blocks of 7 regions around a chosen seed.
Against the per-slide best coverage rule, the local-variance policy's margins were $+0.021$, $+0.034$, and
$+0.011$ for rounds of 1, 5, and 25 and $+0.024$ for blocks, none with $p_H<0.05$ (Appendix~\ref{app:stage1}).
With a single round the policy has no measurements and falls back to its starting rule, the regions nearest the
slide centroid, so the resulting margin of $-0.051$ ($p_H=0.008$) reflects that rule more than the value of
adaptivity. Reconstruction margins were negative in every setting.

($+0.023$ and $+0.035$) and over permuted feedback ($+0.050$ and $+0.084$) did not survive correction
($p_H\ge0.11$). Batched acquisition thus left the detection signal of Q2 intact but did not establish it.

\subsection{Robustness}
The registered stage-2 feedback ablations rest on one permutation. Repeating them with further seeds
(descriptive; Appendix~\ref{app:stage3}) left the conclusions unchanged. Across seeds the mean margin had a standard
deviation of at most 0.007 PCC and 0.013 recall, and the seed-averaged detection margins ($+0.005$, $+0.017$,
$+0.033$, and $+0.074$ at 2--20\%) were close to the registered ones and below their family-wise MDEs. For
reconstruction, the registered seed gave the largest of the three margins at 0.5\% ($+0.031$, against $+0.024$ and
$+0.016$) and was within the range of the others at higher budgets.

Selecting the evaluation genes from the full slide uses information that a prospective experiment would lack.
Selecting them from a random 15\% reference subset changed the stage-1 reconstruction margins by at most 0.003, and
scoring all filtered genes left them negative ($-0.033$ to $-0.079$; Appendix~\ref{app:hvg}). In an exploratory
split, the stage-1 detection margins were larger on the Visium HD slides than on Visium ($+0.048$ against $+0.008$
at 10\%), but with 5 HD studies the smallest attainable $p$ is 0.0625, and platform cannot be separated from study
(Appendix~\ref{app:split}).

\section{Discussion}

The results support a narrower conclusion than a general verdict on adaptive acquisition. For reconstruction, the
tested value-conditioned policies, including one designed to target the imputer's prediction error, did not improve
on a non-adaptive rule chosen on other studies at any budget from 0.5\% to 20\%, whether the comparator was
selected across studies or fixed. The ablations and hybrids separate two parts of this outcome. Measured values
carried usable information: real feedback beat permuted feedback by up to 0.039 PCC when it drove the whole
selection and by up to 0.026 when it chose only the remainder. That information, however, mainly compensated for
leaving a coverage design. A policy that replaced coverage with feedback stayed behind $k$-means and MI-greedy at
low budgets, and one that kept coverage for most of the budget only matched it. This is what the sensor-placement
view predicts, since for reconstruction where measurements are placed should matter more than what they return,
but our evaluation does not test that view directly, and the failed semi-synthetic control prevents us from
claiming that useful value-conditioned reconstruction is impossible.

For detection, the answer depends on the baseline. Feedback raised recall over permuted feedback, and the
local-variance policy beat MI-greedy at 10--20\% (post hoc), but MI-greedy is a design for the other objective. Against the
non-adaptive rule chosen for detection, or against the diversity sampler, the margins were small and uncertain, and
hybrids that spent part of the budget on the detection rule stayed within 0.006 recall of it. Settling whether
feedback adds to the best non-adaptive detection design would need more independent studies than the 18 used here,
especially at 20\%, where the detectable effects are largest.

For practice at the scale tested, a non-adaptive rule chosen on other slides was a sufficient baseline. For
reconstruction, no value-conditioned policy or hybrid exceeded $k$-means at very low budgets or MI-greedy above them
after correction; the largest hybrid margin was $+0.007$ PCC at 2\% ($p_H=0.36$), and only a fixed design,
maximum-uncertainty, exceeded MI-greedy, by 0.003 PCC at 20\% (post hoc). For computationally defined ROIs, a
detection-oriented rule or a diversity design was about as good as feedback, without waiting for measurements
between rounds. Adaptive acquisition methods should be compared with such baselines on the objective they are
meant to serve, and with a version of themselves that receives uninformative values.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The simulator is retrospective: a revealed region returns its measured expression without added noise, batch
effects, or turnaround time, conditions that if anything favour feedback. The ROIs are the 20\% of regions with the
highest local-variance score, not annotated structures. High local variance can mark tissue boundaries, mixtures of
cell types, or technical noise as well as regions a biologist would target, such as tumour margins or immune
niches, so the detection results concern computational heterogeneity and do not show whether feedback helps find a
particular biological target. The acquisition unit is a spot or HD pseudo-spot, smaller than ROIs on platforms
such as GeoMx, and the HD and Visium units differ in spacing. The batch and block simulations approximate but do
not validate any particular platform. The 22-slide results use one imputer, and the $k$-NN and kriging comparisons
rest on 5 slides. The competitor samplers were reimplementations of objectives; SOFisher was run natively only on
its own data and SCR$^2$-ST not at all. The registered tests of the stage-2 feedback ablations use one permutation,
with further seeds only descriptive, and the stage-3 fixed rules were chosen on stage-2 results from the same
slides, which favours the baselines. The feasible comparator,
the residual policy, the feedback ablations, and the hybrids were added after earlier results were known, although
each was registered before its experiments. Budgets above 20\% were not tested.

\section{Conclusion}

Q1: across 22 human slides from 18 studies, the tested value-conditioned policies did not improve whole-section
reconstruction over a feasible non-adaptive rule at any budget from 0.5\% to 20\%, and feedback layered on a
coverage design did not improve on that design. Feedback was informative relative to permuted values but mostly
offset the cost of departing from coverage, and a semi-synthetic control did not establish that the protocol could
detect a reconstruction gain. Q2: whether feedback improves the detection of computationally defined heterogeneous
regions remains unresolved. It helped against a reconstruction-oriented design (post hoc), but no margin over the
non-adaptive rule chosen for detection or over a diversity sampler survived correction. Q3: in exploratory
batched and contiguous settings no detection advantage was established, and revealing regions in rounds of 5 did
not reduce recall. Within this retrospective, spot-level setting, the tested feedback policies offered no
supported advantage over well-chosen non-adaptive placement.

\section{Configuration and implementation checks}
\label{app:repro}

All values are fixed in the released \texttt{config.py}. Seeds: global 2021; random policy $\{0,\dots,4\}$; the
stage-2 permutation for feedback ablation uses the global seed, and stage 3 uses seeds 1 and 2 (1--4 for the
repeated local-variance ablation). Hybrids: coverage
fraction $f\in\{0.5,0.8\}$ of the budget, rounded up. Batched stage-3 runs: rounds of 5 regions. Graphflow: image PCA 64, random Fourier features 512
(median-heuristic bandwidth), ridge 10, graph $k=8$, 20 steps, step size 0.5. $k$-NN imputer: $k=10$, joint image and
space features. Kriging: image PCA 64, ridge 10, residual $k=10$. Placement: feature PCA 64, median-heuristic
lengthscale, noise $10^{-2}$. Error-reduction and residual: neighbourhood 15; the residual policy uses the 256 genes
with the highest variance among profiled regions and 10 profiled neighbours. Local-variance: spatial kernel width 0.1 of
the slide diagonal, exploration weight 0.5. Spiked control: 20 genes, 3 hotspots, radius 0.05 of the diagonal, noise
0.1 of the reference standard deviation. Statistics: 5{,}000 bootstrap resamples; exact sign-flip enumeration over
study clusters; MDE from 300 study resamples with 3{,}000 sign flips each, on a grid of 51 effect sizes.

The code for the larger slides replaces dense distance computations with sparse and incremental ones; a 132-run check
(11 policies, 3 imputers, 2 slide sizes, 2 budgets) reproduced the original code's selections and scores exactly. In
the stage-2 run, the five non-adaptive rules reproduced the stage-1 per-slide scores exactly, and the local-variance and
diversity policies reproduced their stage-1 means. A caching error interrupted the first stage-2 run. For the
results saved before it, a stale cache entry could only have produced a crash, and the error was fixed before any
result was analysed.
