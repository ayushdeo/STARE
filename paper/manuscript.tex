\begin{abstract}
Region-based spatial transcriptomics profiles only part of a tissue section, so the choice of regions to
profile is a budgeted decision. Recent methods make this choice adaptively, conditioning each next region on
the expression already measured. We test whether adaptivity helps under two objectives: reconstructing
expression in the unprofiled regions, and capturing regions of high molecular heterogeneity (ROI detection).
The evaluation uses a simulator built from 22 human HEST-1k slides (7 Visium HD, 15 Visium) from 18 studies
and four tissues, a frozen UNI2-h encoder, a graph-based imputer, and strong non-adaptive placement baselines,
including mutual-information-greedy sensor placement. Analyses were specified before the run, cluster by
study, and correct for 24 primary comparisons. For reconstruction, adaptive policies were worse than the best
non-adaptive baseline at budgets of 0.5--2\% of regions (mean differences $-0.128$ to $-0.040$ PCC over the
top-50 genes; Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$) and within $\pm0.01$ PCC of it at 10--20\%. A policy that selects
directly on measured expression values was worse at every budget ($-0.136$ to $-0.165$). For ROI detection,
the same policy raised recall by $+0.001$ to $+0.042$ over the best coverage baseline. None of these gains
remained significant after correction (adjusted $p\ge0.36$). They were concentrated in the Visium HD slides
and were of similar size to the gain of a non-adaptive diversity sampler. On synthetic data with strong ROI
signal, the protocol detected an adaptivity advantage of $+0.10$ recall, so the null results are not a
property of the simulator alone. In these experiments, the main lever for both objectives was the placement
rule, not adaptivity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Whole-transcriptome spatial assays are costly. Region-of-interest platforms such as GeoMx
DSP~\citep{merritt2020geomx} profile a small, operator-chosen subset of a section, and regions that are not
profiled are reconstructed when needed, usually by predicting expression from co-registered hematoxylin and
eosin (H\&E) images~\citep{jaume2024hest,xie2023bleep}. Which regions are profiled therefore affects two
different things: how much of the section's expression can be recovered, and whether the regions that matter
biologically are measured at all.

Several recent methods treat region choice as a sequential decision. SOFisher~\citep{li2026sofisher} trains a
reinforcement-learning policy that moves fields of view toward regions enriched for a target signal, using the
expression measured so far. SCR$^2$-ST~\citep{zhu2025scr2st} uses reinforcement learning to choose sampling
locations for expression prediction, with a reward dominated by coverage of a single-cell reference manifold.
S2-omics~\citep{yuan2025s2omics} selects ROIs from histology alone, before any measurement, to capture
molecular heterogeneity, and notes that sequential designs that wait for measured expression are hard to run
in practice. The two objectives in these papers differ: SOFisher and S2-omics aim to capture particular
regions, whereas reconstruction aims to predict expression everywhere. An improvement under one objective
need not carry over to the other.

We ask a narrow question under matched conditions. With the image encoder and imputer fixed, and against
strong non-adaptive placement rules, does conditioning region choice on measured expression improve (i)
whole-section reconstruction or (ii) ROI detection, and does any improvement survive acquisition in batches
or contiguous blocks, as real platforms require? For reconstruction, Gaussian-process sensor placement gives
a reason to expect little benefit: with a fixed kernel the posterior variance does not depend on the observed
values, so adaptive and non-adaptive placement share the same optimum~\citep{krause2008near}. For detection,
there is no such argument, since the target set is defined by the values themselves.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A simulator protocol for region acquisition on 22 human HEST-1k slides from 18 studies, with analyses
that resample whole studies and correct for multiple comparisons, specified and committed before the
expanded run. (2) For reconstruction, adaptive policies were worse than the best non-adaptive placement at
low budgets and equivalent to it within 0.01 PCC at 10--20\%; a policy that selects on measured values was
worse at every budget. (3) For ROI detection, value-conditioned selection gave small recall gains that did
not survive correction, were concentrated in one platform, and were matched by a non-adaptive diversity
sampler. Synthetic positive controls show that the protocol can register an adaptivity advantage when one
exists. (4) Code, configuration, and scripts that regenerate every table and figure from the result files.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Predicting expression from histology.}
Methods for predicting spatial expression from H\&E include contrastive retrieval~\citep{xie2023bleep}, flow
matching over spatial neighborhoods~\citep{huang2025stflow}, diffusion models~\citep{zhu2025stem}, and
autoregressive models~\citep{genar2025}, benchmarked on HEST-1k~\citep{jaume2024hest}. We hold the
predictor fixed and study which regions it is given.

\paragraph{Choosing what to measure.}
Gene-panel selectors choose a fixed set of genes~\citep{covert2023persist,reconst2026}. Region selectors
include one-shot rankers that score regions from images~\citep{spatialfinder2026}, histology-based ROI
selection for molecular heterogeneity~\citep{yuan2025s2omics}, and sequential reinforcement-learning
samplers~\citep{li2026sofisher,zhu2025scr2st}. These papers compare against random, uniform, or manual
selection; we add sensor-placement baselines and evaluate both objectives on the same slides.

\paragraph{Active acquisition and sensor placement.}
Active feature acquisition treats measurement as a sequential policy~\citep{yin2020afa}. Variance- and
mutual-information-greedy placement in Gaussian processes are submodular and near-optimal for reconstructing
a spatial field~\citep{krause2008near}; we use them as non-adaptive baselines.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Slides and acquisition unit.}
We use 22 human slides from HEST-1k~\citep{jaume2024hest} across prostate (6), kidney (4), lung (6), and
colon (6); 7 are Visium HD and 15 are standard Visium (Appendix~\ref{app:slides}). Slides were selected by
rules fixed in advance: human, whole-transcriptome, at least 1{,}000 spots under tissue, source image at most
0.6\,$\mu$m/px, one slide per specimen, and at most two slides per study. The 22 slides come from 18 HEST
studies. The acquisition unit (``region'') is one native spot. For Visium, spots are 55\,$\mu$m in diameter
on a 100\,$\mu$m pitch; the Visium HD slides are distributed by HEST as aggregated pseudo-spots with a
median spacing of 128\,$\mu$m on our slides. Coordinates are converted to microns with each slide's
estimated pixel size. Slides contain 1{,}766 to 9{,}080 regions. Each region's image input is the HEST
224\,px patch at 0.5\,$\mu$m/px centred on the spot, embedded with frozen UNI2-h~\citep{chen2024uni}.
Expression is log1p-transformed; genes detected in fewer than 10\% of regions are removed.

\paragraph{Simulator and objectives.}
A policy reveals regions up to a budget given as a fraction of the slide's regions (0.5\% to 20\%). A revealed
region exposes its measured expression. For \emph{reconstruction}, a fixed imputer predicts the unprofiled
regions from the profiled ones, and we score the per-gene Pearson correlation over unprofiled regions,
averaged over the top-50 highly variable genes (PCC), following the HEST-Benchmark target~\citep{jaume2024hest}.
For \emph{ROI detection}, we define ROIs from ground truth as the 20\% of regions with the highest local
molecular heterogeneity (variance of top-50-gene expression across a region's 8 nearest spatial neighbours,
averaged over genes), in the spirit of S2-omics~\citep{yuan2025s2omics}, and score recall: the fraction of ROI
regions that were profiled. ROIs and gene sets are used only for evaluation.

\paragraph{Imputer.}
The primary imputer (graphflow) maps image principal components through 512 random Fourier features, fits a
ridge regression to expression on the profiled regions, and then runs 20 steps of message passing on a fixed
8-nearest-neighbour graph in image-and-space features, holding profiled regions at their measured values.
Its predictions depend on the measured values. Its uncertainty, one minus the graph influence reaching a
region from profiled nodes, depends on which regions are profiled but not on their values. A $k$-nearest
neighbour and a regression-kriging imputer were run on the earlier slide set only (Appendix~\ref{app:imputers}).

\paragraph{Policies.}
Non-adaptive: random (5 seeds), farthest-point in space (grid), $k$-means on image embeddings with medoids,
a reimplementation of a one-shot image ranker~\citep{spatialfinder2026}, and variance-greedy and
mutual-information (MI)-greedy placement with an RBF kernel on joint image-and-coordinate
features~\citep{krause2008near}. Sequential: \emph{error-reduction} selects the region whose 15 nearest
unprofiled neighbours in the imputer's feature space have the largest summed imputer uncertainty; \emph{maximum-uncertainty} selects the
region of highest imputer uncertainty. Under graphflow both depend on the profiled set but not on the measured
values. \emph{Value-conditioned} selection scores each candidate by the standardized kernel-weighted local variance
of the measured expression around it plus 0.5 times its standardized distance to the nearest profiled region,
and starts from the region nearest the slide centroid; its choices depend on the measured values, so it is the
direct test of value-adaptivity. Two further policies
implement the stated objectives of prior samplers in our simulator: a \emph{diversity} sampler (farthest-point
in joint image and space, after SCR$^2$-ST~\citep{zhu2025scr2st}, non-adaptive) and an \emph{ROI-seeking}
sampler (moves toward measured regions with large expression deviation, after SOFisher~\citep{li2026sofisher},
adaptive). These are reimplementations of the objectives, not the authors' trained models.

\paragraph{Comparators and statistics.}
For reconstruction, the comparator on each slide is the best of the five non-adaptive policies other than
random (``best non-adaptive''). For detection and the realism experiments, it is the best of $k$-means, grid,
and MI-greedy (``best coverage''). Taking the per-slide maximum favours the baseline. For each margin
(policy minus comparator, per slide) we report the mean, a 95\% percentile bootstrap CI that resamples whole
studies (5{,}000 resamples), and an exact two-sided sign-flip test that flips the signs of whole studies
(all $2^{18}$ patterns). Slides from the same study are therefore not treated as independent. Before the run
we fixed a primary family of 24 margins (reconstruction and detection at each budget, and the realism
settings) and adjust its $p$-values with Holm's method~\citep{holm1979simple} ($p_H$). The decision rules,
also fixed in advance, were: an adaptivity advantage is claimed only for a positive margin with $p_H<0.05$.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Synthetic controls: can the protocol register an adaptivity advantage?}
We first checked that the simulator and metrics can show an advantage when one exists, using synthetic
$20\times20$ slides with image-unpredictable, spatially clustered structure (8 slides per setting, 10\%
budget, $k$-NN imputer; Figure~\ref{fig:controls}). For reconstruction, we added a non-deployable
\emph{difficulty oracle} that orders regions by true local heterogeneity weighted by distance to regions
already chosen. It did not exceed the best coverage baseline at any structure length: its margin was $-0.105$
and $-0.066$ PCC at the two shortest correlation lengths and between $-0.013$ and $-0.020$ at the three
longer ones, with every CI below zero (Figure~\ref{fig:controls}a). For detection, the value-conditioned policy's recall margin over the
best coverage baseline rose with the ROI signal amplitude $\alpha$, from $-0.045$ at $\alpha=0$ to $+0.052$
(95\% CI $+0.033$ to $+0.071$) at $\alpha=4$ and $+0.101$ ($+0.065$ to $+0.135$) at $\alpha=8$, positive on
all 8 slides in both settings (Figure~\ref{fig:controls}b). The error-reduction policy, which targets the
imputer's uncertainty (here the variance of measured expression among neighbours) rather than local
heterogeneity, stayed at or below zero. The protocol can therefore register a value-adaptivity advantage for
detection when the ROI signal is strong, and in this setting even ground-truth-informed ordering did not help
reconstruction.

\subsection{Reconstruction on 22 slides}
Table~\ref{tab:recon} gives absolute PCC. The best non-adaptive baseline reached 0.470 at 0.5\% budget and
0.709 at 20\%; MI-greedy alone reached 0.412 and 0.707. Table~\ref{tab:primary} and
Figure~\ref{fig:main}a give the paired margins. The error-reduction policy was below the best non-adaptive
baseline by 0.128, 0.083, and 0.040 PCC at 0.5\%, 1\%, and 2\% ($p_H<0.001$; lower in all 18 studies at 0.5\% and
1\% and in 17 of 18 at 2\%) and by 0.011 at 5\% ($p_H=0.057$). At 10\% and 20\% the margins were $-0.002$; their 90\% study-cluster CIs,
$[-0.006, +0.001]$ and $[-0.003, +0.001]$, lie within $\pm0.01$ PCC, so the two are equivalent at that
tolerance (an equivalence margin we chose after seeing the data). The maximum-uncertainty policy behaved
similarly; at 20\% it was $+0.001$ above the best non-adaptive baseline (95\% CI $-0.001$ to $+0.003$,
unadjusted $p=0.35$). The value-conditioned policy, the one that selects on measured values, was below the
best coverage baseline at every budget, by 0.136 to 0.165 PCC ($p_H<0.001$), reaching 0.544 at 20\% against
about 0.70 for the coverage policies.

\subsection{ROI detection on 22 slides}
Table~\ref{tab:detect} gives absolute recall. Coverage policies captured ROIs at about the rate expected from
the budget (recall 0.200--0.232 at 20\%), and the ground-truth ordering reached 0.999. The value-conditioned
policy reached 0.278. Its margins over the best coverage baseline were $+0.001$, $+0.013$, $+0.021$, and
$+0.042$ at 2\%, 5\%, 10\%, and 20\% (Table~\ref{tab:primary}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}b). The unadjusted
$p$-values at 5--20\% were 0.040--0.044, and after Holm adjustment over the primary family all were 0.36. By
the pre-specified rule, these margins do not establish a detection advantage.

Two further comparisons bear on how to read the small positive estimates. First, the non-adaptive diversity
sampler improved recall over the best coverage baseline by a similar amount: $+0.020$ (95\% CI $+0.009$ to
$+0.031$) at 10\% and $+0.029$ ($+0.018$ to $+0.042$) at 20\%, with Holm-adjusted $p$ of 0.015 and 0.002
within that experiment, where the value-conditioned margins had adjusted $p=0.36$
(Table~\ref{tab:detect}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}b). Second, the detection margins were concentrated in the
Visium HD slides: at 10\% and 20\% they were $+0.048$ and $+0.099$ on HD (7 slides, 5 studies, the smallest
attainable $p$ with 5 clusters is 0.0625) and $+0.008$ and $+0.015$ on Visium (15 slides, 13 studies;
$p=0.43$ and $0.47$; Appendix~\ref{app:split}). The ROI-seeking sampler did not improve recall
($-0.012$ to $-0.005$, all CIs including zero) and reduced reconstruction PCC by 0.19 to 0.28.

\subsection{Batched and contiguous acquisition}
Real platforms acquire regions in rounds, and ROIs are contiguous. At 10\% budget we revealed regions in
rounds of 1, 5, or 25, or in a single round, with the policy using only measurements available at the start
of each round, and separately in contiguous blocks of 7 regions around a policy-chosen seed
(Table~\ref{tab:realism}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}c). Reconstruction margins were negative in every setting
($p_H<0.001$). Detection margins were $+0.021$, $+0.034$, and $+0.011$ for rounds of 1, 5, and 25, and
$+0.024$ for contiguous blocks, none with $p_H<0.05$ (rounds of 5: $p_H=0.073$). With a single round the
policy has no measurements and falls back to its starting rule, which takes the regions nearest the slide
centroid; this compact block captured fewer ROIs than the coverage policies (margin $-0.051$, $p_H=0.008$).

\subsection{Robustness}
\emph{Gene set.} Selecting the top-50 genes from the full slide uses information that a prospective
experiment would not have. Selecting them instead from a random 15\% reference subset gave reconstruction
margins within 0.003 of the full-slide choice, and scoring all filtered genes gave smaller but still negative
margins ($-0.033$ to $-0.079$; all CIs below zero; Appendix~\ref{app:hvg}). \emph{Tissue.} The value-conditioned
reconstruction margins were negative in all four tissues at every budget, as were the error-reduction margins
at 0.5--5\%; at 10--20\% the error-reduction margin in kidney was $+0.001$ and $+0.000$. The detection margins
were positive in kidney, lung, and colon and between $-0.006$ and $-0.001$ in prostate at 2--10\%
(Appendix~\ref{app:split}). \emph{Native SOFisher.} We ran the authors' SOFisher code on its bundled mouse cortex data
for 5 million steps; the evaluation reward for captured target regions rose from a median of $-238$ early in
training to $+147$ late (Appendix~\ref{app:sofisher}), confirming that the method optimizes target-region capture.
We could not run SCR$^2$-ST natively because its processed training data are not publicly available.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Reconstruction.} The reconstruction results agree with the sensor-placement argument. With the
imputer's uncertainty independent of measured values, the sequential policies are in effect alternative
fixed designs, and they matched the best fixed design at moderate budgets and fell behind it at low budgets,
where coverage matters most. Selecting on measured values moved measurements toward locally variable areas
and away from coverage, which lowered PCC by 0.14 to 0.17. On synthetic data, ordering by true local
difficulty did not help either. Whole-section reconstruction rewards spreading measurements across the
section, and adaptivity in these experiments only redistributed measurements away from that.

\paragraph{Detection.} Detection is the objective under which adaptivity could help, and the synthetic
control shows that it does when the ROI signal in measured expression is strong. On real slides the estimated
gain was at most 0.04 recall at 20\% budget, against a gap of about 0.72 to the ground-truth ordering, and it
did not survive correction. A non-adaptive diversity design gave a similar gain, so the part of the gain we
can resolve is not specific to adaptivity. The concentration in Visium HD slides may reflect the smoother
pseudo-spot signal or the particular studies; with 5 HD studies we cannot separate these.

\paragraph{Practical implication.} For budgets and slides like these, a placement rule computed in advance
(MI-greedy or $k$-means) was as accurate for reconstruction as any adaptive policy we tested. For detection, a
non-adaptive diversity design reached recall within 0.012 of the value-conditioned policy (0.266 against
0.278 at 20\% budget) without waiting for measurements between rounds.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The simulator is retrospective: a revealed region returns its measured expression
without additional noise, batch effects, or turnaround time, which favours adaptive policies. The acquisition
unit is a spot or HD pseudo-spot, smaller than typical GeoMx ROIs, and the HD and Visium units differ in
spacing. The 22-slide results use one imputer; the kNN and kriging comparisons rest on 5 slides. The ROI
definition is one choice among several, and ROIs from expert annotation could behave differently. The prior
samplers were evaluated as reimplementations of their objectives in our simulator, not as the authors'
trained systems; SOFisher was run natively only on its own data, and SCR$^2$-ST not at all. The equivalence
tolerance of 0.01 PCC was chosen after the data were seen. Budgets above 20\% were not tested.

\section{Conclusion}

Across 22 human slides from 18 studies, adaptive region acquisition did not improve whole-section
reconstruction over strong non-adaptive placement: it was worse at low budgets and equivalent within 0.01 PCC
at 10--20\%, and selecting on measured values was worse at every budget. For ROI detection, value-conditioned
selection produced small recall gains that did not survive correction for multiple comparisons and were
matched by a non-adaptive diversity design. The same protocol registered a clear detection advantage on
synthetic data with strong ROI signal, which marks the setting in which adaptivity is worth testing again:
targets that are invisible in histology but strongly expressed once measured.

\section{Configuration}
\label{app:repro}

All values are fixed in the released \texttt{config.py}. Seeds: global 2021; random policy $\{0,\dots,4\}$.
Imputer (graphflow): image PCA 64, random Fourier features 512 (median-heuristic bandwidth), ridge 10, graph
$k=8$, 20 steps, step size 0.5. kNN imputer: $k=10$, joint image and space features. Kriging: image PCA 64, ridge
10, residual $k=10$. Placement: feature PCA 64, median-heuristic lengthscale, noise $10^{-2}$. Error-reduction
neighbourhood 15. Value-conditioned: spatial kernel width 0.1 of the slide diagonal, exploration weight 0.5;
the ROI-seeking sampler uses the same values. Detection ROIs: top 20\% by heterogeneity over 8 spatial
neighbours. Statistics: 5{,}000 bootstrap resamples, exact sign-flip enumeration over study clusters, Holm over
24 primary margins. The code used for the larger slides replaces dense distance computations with sparse and
incremental ones; on a 132-run check (11 policies, 3 imputers, 2 slide sizes, 2 budgets) it reproduced the
original code's selections and scores exactly.
