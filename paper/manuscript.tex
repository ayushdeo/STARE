\begin{abstract}
Spatial transcriptomics is costly, so many studies profile only selected regions of a section, and recent methods
choose each next region from expression already measured. A recent benchmark of active spot selection for
training predictors across patients reported inconsistent gains over random sampling; we ask instead whether, within
a slide, measured feedback improves selection over a fixed design matched to the objective. We built a
retrospective simulator from 22 human HEST-1k slides (18 studies, four tissues), fixed the image encoder and
imputer, registered four analysis stages in timestamped commits before running them, and drew inference at the
study level. Each feedback policy was compared with a non-adaptive design chosen on held-out studies and with
itself fed uninformative values. \textbf{RQ1:} for whole-section reconstruction, a policy targeting the imputer's
error was below the fixed design at every budget up to 10\%, by up to 0.092 Pearson correlation and significantly
at 0.5 and 1\%, both on its own unprofiled regions and on a common held-out set. \textbf{RQ2:} for capturing
heterogeneous regions, the registered policy did not detectably beat a design chosen for detection, and a
diversity sampler that uses no feedback did about as well; with its settings chosen on the other studies, the same
policy beat the design by 0.016 to 0.049 recall at 2--10\%, but not detectably the diversity sampler. \textbf{RQ3:} spending half or more of the budget on a
fixed design and the rest on feedback never beat that design; from 2\% upward every hybrid was within 0.007
correlation and 0.006 recall of it, a null with at least 80\% power to detect gains of 0.02 and 0.03 at 2--10\%.
\textbf{RQ4} (exploratory): batches of five neither eroded nor established a detection advantage. With
300\,$\mu$m tiles no feedback policy beat the fixed design, a null that could miss gains below about 0.04 to 0.12.
Within simulated budgets, one imputer, and computational endpoints, the feedback policies we tested lost to a
well-chosen fixed design for reconstruction, where neither positive control showed that a gain could be detected;
for detection, a tuned feedback policy beat the registered fixed designs but not detectably a diversity design.
Adaptive acquisition methods should be benchmarked against the strongest objective-matched fixed designs, against
themselves without informative feedback, and with settings chosen on held-out data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Spatial transcriptomics measures gene expression without dissociating tissue, so expression can be read in its
histological context~\citep{stahl2016visualization,rao2021exploring,moses2022museum}. Whole-transcriptome assays
remain costly, and region-of-interest platforms such as GeoMx DSP~\citep{merritt2020geomx} profile only selected
regions of a section. Expression elsewhere can be predicted from the co-registered hematoxylin and eosin (H\&E)
image~\citep{xie2023bleep,jaume2024hest}. Which regions are measured therefore determines both how well the
unmeasured tissue can be reconstructed and whether distinct tissue compartments are measured at all.

Choosing regions is an experimental-design problem, and recent methods solve it sequentially.
SOFisher~\citep{li2026sofisher} uses reinforcement learning to move fields of view toward regions enriched for a
target signal, and SCR$^2$-ST~\citep{zhu2026scr2st} learns where to sample spots to build training sets for
expression prediction. S2-omics~\citep{yuan2025s2omics} takes the opposite approach and selects regions from
histology alone, before any measurement. The appeal of sequential selection follows active learning~\citep{settles2009active}: each measurement
can inform the next. Its cost is a measurement turnaround between choices. A recent benchmark of active spot
selection for training expression predictors found inconsistent gains over random sampling at small
budgets~\citep{zhu2026benchmarking}, but addressed a different decision (Section~\ref{sec:related}). We are not
aware of an evaluation that compares feedback-driven region selection within a slide against fixed designs, whose
selections do not depend on measured values, matched to the objective while ablating the measured feedback itself.

Theory gives different expectations for different goals. For reconstructing a Gaussian-process field with a
known kernel, posterior variance does not depend on the observed values, so a design fixed before any measurement
is as good as a sequential one; when the model's parameters are uncertain, sequential designs can do
better~\citep{krause2008near}. Imputers learned from images are not Gaussian processes with known kernels, so for
reconstruction the question is empirical. Finding regions defined by their expression is closer to active search,
in which each choice exploits the labels already observed to locate further targets~\citep{garnett2012bayesian}.

Beating random selection does not show that feedback helps, because a policy can win through its selection rule
alone. An evaluation that can separate these cases needs four features. The baseline must be a design that could be
chosen before the target slide is profiled, because selecting the best design on the evaluation data biases the
comparison~\citep{varma2006bias,cawley2010over}. Generalisation must be assessed across studies, not within
them~\citep{bernau2014cross}, with slides from one study treated as dependent~\citep{field2007bootstrapping}. The
contribution of feedback must be isolated by giving the same policy uninformative values, in the spirit of
permutation tests for learning algorithms~\citep{ojala2010permutation}. Finally, null results need positive
controls and power analysis to be interpretable~\citep{altman1995absence}.

We built such an evaluation and asked four questions. \textbf{RQ1}: does conditioning region selection on
measured expression improve whole-section reconstruction over a feasible non-adaptive design? \textbf{RQ2}: does
it improve the capture of molecularly heterogeneous regions? \textbf{RQ3}: does feedback add to a strong fixed
design when it allocates only the remainder of the budget? \textbf{RQ4}, an exploratory stress test: does a
detection advantage persist when measurements arrive in batches? Across four registered stages, no feedback policy
beat a well-chosen fixed design for reconstruction, which was significantly better at small budgets even when all
policies were scored on the same held-out regions. For detection, the registered policy did not beat a design
chosen for detection, but a version tuned on other studies did, modestly, though not detectably better than a
diversity sampler that uses no feedback. We contribute (i) a reproducible
simulator on 22 human slides from 18 studies with feasible, objective-matched baselines, feedback ablations, and
study-level inference; (ii) a registered negative result for reconstruction and a qualified positive result for
detection, made interpretable by positive controls and minimum detectable effects; and (iii) an account of the
result: the imputer's uncertainty depends only on where regions are profiled, so feedback can aid reconstruction
only through its predictions, and for detection a diversity sampler without feedback finds heterogeneous regions
about as well as the untuned policy.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Expression from histology.} Expression has been predicted from H\&E by contrastive
retrieval~\citep{xie2023bleep}, flow matching over spatial neighbourhoods~\citep{huang2025stflow},
diffusion~\citep{zhu2025stem}, and autoregressive generation~\citep{genar2025}, and benchmarked on
HEST-1k~\citep{jaume2024hest}. These methods take the measured regions as given. We hold the predictor fixed and
vary only the regions it receives.

\paragraph{Choosing what to measure.} Pool-based active learning selects queries by uncertainty~\citep{lewis1994sequential},
expected error reduction~\citep{roy2001toward}, or coverage of the input space~\citep{sener2018active}. Sensor
placement offers the matching non-adaptive designs: variance- and mutual-information-greedy selection, which are
near-optimal for Gaussian-process reconstruction~\citep{krause2008near}. Adaptive submodularity extends such
guarantees to greedy policies that condition on observations~\citep{golovin2011adaptive}, and active search
addresses the related goal of collecting positives~\citep{garnett2012bayesian}. In batch acquisition, the members
of a batch are chosen without each other's labels~\citep{kirsch2019batchbald}, and global optimisation commonly
begins with a space-filling design before switching to adaptive queries~\citep{jones1998efficient}. Active feature acquisition casts measurement as a
sequential policy~\citep{yin2020afa}.

\paragraph{Design in spatial omics.} Gene-panel selectors fix which genes to
measure~\citep{covert2023persist,reconst2026}. Region selectors range from image-based rankers such as SpatialFinder,
which score regions from H\&E before any expression is measured~\citep{spatialfinder2026}, and histology-based heterogeneity
selection~\citep{yuan2025s2omics} to
sequential reinforcement-learning samplers~\citep{li2026sofisher,zhu2026scr2st}. Our evaluation includes
reimplementations of these objectives and a native run of SOFisher. The closest evaluation is a retrospective
benchmark of active spot selection on breast and skin cancer cohorts~\citep{zhu2026benchmarking}, which compared
uncertainty- and diversity-based strategies with random sampling and found that active strategies did not
consistently win at small budgets. Its question differs from ours in three ways. It selects spots to train a
morphology-to-expression predictor that is evaluated on held-out patients, whereas we select regions within a
slide for a fixed imputer. Its baseline is random sampling, whereas ours are non-adaptive designs chosen for each
objective. It compares selection strategies, whereas we hold each strategy fixed and remove the information in the
measured values.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Acquisition simulator}
A slide is a set of regions, each with an image embedding, spatial coordinates, and measured expression. A
policy reveals regions one at a time until a budget, a fixed fraction of the slide's regions, is spent; each
revealed region exposes its measured expression to the policy. The simulator is retrospective, as in pool-based
active-learning evaluation~\citep{settles2009active}: every region has been measured, so any selection can be
scored against ground truth. For RQ4, regions are revealed in rounds, and within a round the policy can use only
measurements available at its start. Budgets range from 0.5\% of regions (9 to 45 regions depending on slide
size, comparable to ROI-based profiling) to 20\%.

\subsection{Slides and preprocessing}
We used 22 human HEST-1k slides~\citep{jaume2024hest} from prostate (6), kidney (4), lung (6), and colon (6), of
which 7 are Visium HD~\citep{oliveira2025visiumhd} and 15 standard Visium~\citep{stahl2016visualization}
(Appendix~\ref{app:slides}). The tissues are the four used to evaluate the image-based ranker we compare
against~\citep{spatialfinder2026}. Selection rules were fixed before any result on these slides: human tissue,
whole-transcriptome assay, at least 1{,}000 spots under tissue, a source image of at most 0.6\,$\mu$m/px so that
image patches are not substantially upsampled, one slide per specimen, and at most two slides per study. The
slides come from 18 studies, which serve as the independent units of inference.

A region is one native spot, the unit at which HEST provides both expression and an image patch. Visium spots are
55\,$\mu$m wide on a 100\,$\mu$m pitch. HEST distributes Visium HD data as 128\,$\mu$m square pseudo-spots pooled
from 16\,$\mu$m bins, the default of its Visium HD reader,\footnote{\url{https://hest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/hest.readers.VisiumHDReader.html}}
so an HD region covers about seven times the area of a Visium spot. Slides contain 1{,}766 to 9{,}080 regions. Each region's image input is the HEST
224\,px patch at 0.5\,$\mu$m/px~\citep{jaume2024hest}, embedded with UNI2-h, a later release of the pathology
foundation model UNI~\citep{chen2024uni},\footnote{\url{https://huggingface.co/MahmoodLab/UNI2-h}} used frozen.
Following the HEST benchmark, expression is log1p-transformed and genes detected in fewer than 10\% of regions are
removed~\citep{jaume2024hest}.

\subsection{Objectives}
\emph{Reconstruction} (RQ1, RQ3): a fixed imputer predicts the unprofiled regions, scored by the per-gene Pearson
correlation over those regions averaged over the 50 most variable genes (PCC), the HEST benchmark
target~\citep{jaume2024hest}. \emph{Detection} (RQ2--RQ4): regions of interest (ROIs) are the 20\% of regions with
the highest local molecular heterogeneity, which we define as the variance of top-50-gene expression across each
region's 8 nearest spatial neighbours. The definition is ours; it is motivated by the goal of capturing molecular
heterogeneity that drives S2-omics~\citep{yuan2025s2omics}, which itself selects regions from histology. The ROI
fraction equals
the largest budget, so a perfect policy could capture every ROI. We score recall, the fraction of ROIs profiled;
an oracle that profiles regions in order of true heterogeneity bounds it from above.
Gene sets and ROIs are computed from ground truth and used only for scoring.

\subsection{Imputer}
One imputer serves every policy, so differences between policies reflect selection alone. It maps image principal
components through random Fourier features~\citep{rahimi2007random}, fits a ridge regression to the profiled
regions, and propagates the resulting predictions over a fixed 8-nearest-neighbour graph in image and spatial
features while holding profiled regions at their measured values, as in harmonic label
propagation~\citep{zhu2003semi}. Its use of spatial neighbourhoods is inspired by predictors such as
STFlow~\citep{huang2025stflow}, but it is far simpler, which makes it cheap enough to refit after every
measurement. Its predictions depend on the
measured values. Its uncertainty, one minus the graph influence that reaches a region from profiled nodes, depends
only on which regions are profiled. A $k$-nearest-neighbour imputer and a regression-kriging
imputer~\citep{hengl2007regression} were evaluated on an earlier slide set (Appendix~\ref{app:imputers}).
Hyperparameters of the imputer and policies were fixed before the registered analysis in which each was first
used and were not tuned on its outcomes, except in the registered stage-4 tuning of local-variance
(Section~\ref{sec:s4methods}); those of the imputer were set during development on one prostate slide
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\subsection{Policies}
A \emph{fixed design} is one whose selections do not depend on measured expression; a \emph{feedback policy} is one
whose selections do.

\emph{Non-adaptive designs}, fixed designs computed from images and coordinates alone. Random; farthest-point selection in space (grid), a greedy $k$-centre
cover~\citep{gonzalez1985clustering}; $k$-means~\citep{lloyd1982least} on image embeddings, taking the region
nearest each centroid; a one-shot ranker after SpatialFinder~\citep{spatialfinder2026}, which scores regions once
from image embeddings by rare-cluster membership and distance to the cluster centroid, a simplification of its
diversity score without the cell classifier or pathologist labelling (``one-shot'' below); and variance- and
mutual-information (MI)-greedy placement~\citep{krause2008near} with an RBF kernel on image and coordinate
features and a median-heuristic lengthscale~\citep{garreau2017large}. Grid, $k$-means, and MI-greedy are the
\emph{coverage designs} of the stage-1 analyses.

\emph{Sequential fixed designs.} Error-reduction~\citep{roy2001toward} picks the region whose 15 nearest
unprofiled neighbours in the imputer's feature space carry the most uncertainty, and maximum-uncertainty picks the
most uncertain region~\citep{lewis1994sequential}. Because the imputer's uncertainty does not depend on measured
values, both are fixed designs computed one region at a time; for error-reduction we confirmed that permuted values
leave its selections unchanged.

\emph{Feedback policies.} The \emph{residual} policy keeps the error-reduction form but replaces the
uncertainty with an estimate of the imputer's actual error. It computes the leave-one-out residual of the image
ridge model at each profiled region from the hat matrix~\citep{hastie2009elements}, averages it over each unprofiled
region's 10 nearest profiled neighbours, and multiplies by one minus the graph influence. The
\emph{local-variance} policy, our detection policy, scores a candidate by the kernel-weighted variance of measured
expression around it plus 0.5 times its distance to the nearest profiled region, an exploitation term for
heterogeneity balanced by an exploration term as in active search~\citep{garnett2012bayesian}. While no region has
been measured, it selects the region nearest the slide centroid.

\emph{Competitor objectives.} A diversity sampler applies farthest-point selection in joint image and spatial
features, the coverage criterion of core-set active learning~\citep{sener2018active}. It approximates the coverage
and diversity terms of the SCR$^2$-ST reward, which rewards covering single-cell states, cell-type diversity, and
spatial spread~\citep{zhu2026scr2st}. Although it is a fixed design, it was registered as a competitor, not as a
candidate for the feasible design below. An adaptive ROI-seeking sampler follows the reward of
SOFisher~\citep{li2026sofisher}. Both reimplement stated objectives, not the authors' trained models.

\emph{Hybrids} (RQ3). A hybrid takes its first $\lceil fn\rceil$ regions, for a budget of $n$ regions and
$f\in\{0.5,0.8\}$ (the 50\% and 80\% hybrids), from a non-adaptive \emph{base design} and the rest from a feedback
policy: the residual policy for reconstruction and local-variance for detection. The base design is the same for
every slide at each budget and objective: $k$-means at 0.5--1\% and MI-greedy at 2--20\% for reconstruction, and
variance-greedy at 2--10\% and the one-shot ranker at 20\% for detection, chosen from stage-2 results as described
under Comparators. This mirrors the space-filling start of adaptive global optimisation~\citep{jones1998efficient}.
Because the base designs select in a predetermined order, the first phase is exactly the prefix of the design's own
selection (verified for all four designs at both fractions), so a hybrid and its base design differ only in how the
last $1-f$ of the budget is spent.

\subsection{Feedback ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}
Each feedback policy and hybrid was also run with uninformative feedback while its selection rule was
left unchanged. Under \emph{permuted} feedback, profiling a region reveals the expression of a randomly chosen
other region, so the residual policy's leave-one-out model, for example, is fitted to permuted values. Under
\emph{constant} feedback, every region appears to have the slide's mean expression. The imputer and the scores
always use the true values. A policy that gains from feedback should outperform its own permuted version, the logic
of permutation tests for whether a learner exploits real structure~\citep{ojala2010permutation}. Stage 2 used one
permutation; stage 3 used two per configuration and repeated the stage-2 ablations with up to four further seeds.
Constant feedback cannot mislead, but its effect depends on the rule. Local-variance keeps its exploration term and
becomes a coverage rule, so its constant version isolates what the values add. For the residual policy every
leave-one-out residual becomes zero, all candidates tie, and the policy selects regions in index order, so its
constant version is an arbitrary placement rather than a control (checked post hoc).

\subsection{Comparators}
The primary stage-2 comparator is a \emph{feasible} design chosen without the target slide. For each held-out
study, the non-adaptive design (grid, $k$-means, variance-greedy, MI-greedy, or one-shot) with the best mean score
on the other 17 studies at that budget and objective is applied to the held-out slides, a leave-one-study-out
form of cross-study validation~\citep{bernau2014cross}. The \emph{hindsight benchmark}, the best non-adaptive design
for each slide chosen after seeing that slide's result, is reported only for reference, since selecting on the evaluation
data inflates the comparator~\citep{varma2006bias,cawley2010over}. Stage 3 compared each hybrid with its base
design, the design chosen as feasible for most held-out studies at that budget and objective. The base designs were
chosen on stage-2 results from the same slides, which favours the baseline.

\subsection{Statistical analysis and registration}
Each margin is a per-slide difference between two policies. We report its mean with a 95\% percentile bootstrap
interval~\citep{efron1993introduction} that resamples whole studies~\citep{field2007bootstrapping} (5{,}000
resamples), and an exact two-sided sign-flip test over the $2^{18}$ study-level sign
patterns~\citep{winkler2014permutation}. Because resampling as few as 18 clusters can give intervals that are too
narrow~\citep{cameron2008bootstrap}, we also report, post hoc, intervals obtained by inverting the sign-flip test
(Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). Intervals are not adjusted for multiplicity; $p_H$ denotes $p$-values
adjusted by Holm's procedure within a registered family~\citep{holm1979simple}. To make null results
interpretable~\citep{altman1995absence}, we report the minimum detectable effect~\citep{bloom1995minimum}: the
smallest true mean margin detected with 80\% power~\citep{cohen1988statistical}, estimated by simulation that
resamples studies around the observed dispersion~\citep{arnold2011simulation}. For a family of $m$ margins we
compute it at $\alpha=0.05/m$ (MDE$_H$), a conservative bound for the Holm decision.

The sign-flip test assumes exchangeable study margins. This holds for a comparator that is the same for every study but not exactly for the
feasible one, whose selection for each study uses the other 17. Following the principle that model selection must
be repeated inside resampling~\citep{varma2006bias}, we checked the stage-2 results post hoc with a bootstrap that
repeats the selection in every replicate and with MI-greedy as the comparator on every slide
(Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}).

Analyses were registered in the project's version-controlled repository in four
stages~\citep{nosek2018preregistration}, each before its experiments and after the previous stage's results were
known.\footnote{Verbatim excerpts of the four plans and their commit dates are in the supplementary material
(\texttt{registered\_plans/}); the plans label RQ4 as Q3.} Stage 1 compared policies with hindsight
benchmarks in a family of 24 margins, including the first batched experiments and contiguous blocks of 7 regions
around a chosen seed, which approximate field-of-view acquisition (Appendix~\ref{app:stage1}). Stage 2 introduced
the feasible design, the residual policy, and the feedback ablations, with a primary family
of 18 margins: residual minus feasible at six budgets (RQ1), and at four budgets local-variance minus its permuted
version, minus the feasible design, and minus the diversity sampler (RQ2). Stage 3 tested the hybrids, with a
primary family of 20 margins, each hybrid minus its base design run for the whole budget (RQ3), and a
secondary family of 24 that compared hybrids with their permuted versions and repeated the batched experiments
with the base detection design and permuted controls (RQ4). Stage 4 (Section~\ref{sec:s4methods}) had primary
families of 12 margins on a common evaluation set (residual and 50\% reconstruction hybrid against their
comparators at six budgets), 9 at tile scale, and 4 for tuning, and a secondary family of 30 with the MSE,
detection-hybrid, and constant-feedback contrasts. In every stage a benefit was to be claimed only for a
positive margin with $p_H<0.05$. Results are labelled confirmatory (registered primary), secondary (registered,
Holm within the secondary family), descriptive, or post hoc.

\subsection{Positive controls}
A null result is informative only if the protocol could have registered an effect. In synthetic slides of
$20\times20$ regions with spatially clustered structure that the image cannot predict (8 slides per setting, 10\%
budget, $k$-NN imputer), we varied the correlation length of the hidden structure and the amplitude
$a_{\mathrm{ROI}}$ with which ROIs appear in measured expression. Synthetic margins are taken over the hindsight
benchmark of the simple designs: the better of grid and $k$-means on each slide, and for detection also random. On
the 22 real slides we injected 20 genes carrying three spatial hotspots of radius 5\% of the slide diagonal, in the
manner of simulated spatially variable genes~\citep{svensson2018spatialde}, with standard deviation equal to a
spike amplitude $a_{\mathrm{spike}}\in\{0,1,2\}$ times the median of the slide's top-50 genes. Because the image
cannot predict the hotspots, only measured values reveal them. Before running this control we set its success
criterion: the residual policy beats the feasible design with an interval excluding zero at $a_{\mathrm{spike}}=2$.

\subsection{Stage-4 protocol}
\label{sec:s4methods}
Stage 4 addressed four limits of the earlier stages. \emph{Common evaluation set}: a fixed random 20\% of each
slide's regions was withheld from selection, and every policy was scored on it by PCC and mean squared error (MSE)
over the top-50 genes; budgets count all regions. \emph{Tiles}: spots were pooled into 300\,$\mu$m squares with at
least three spots (mean expression and embedding), budgets were 12, 24, and 48 tiles, the ROIs were the 20\% of
tiles with the highest within-tile variance of the top-50 genes, and policies ran one tile at a time or in 2 or 4
rounds, with 20\% of tiles withheld for reconstruction. \emph{Tuning}: under the stage-2 protocol, local-variance
was run with exploration weight $\{0.25, 0.5, 1, 2\}$ and kernel width $\{0.05, 0.1, 0.2\}$ of the slide diagonal,
and for each held-out study the setting with the best mean recall on the other studies was applied, as for the
feasible design. \emph{Headroom}: on one slide per tissue at 0.5--2\%, a greedy selection added, among 48 random
candidates, the region that most increased held-out PCC computed with the true expression. It selects on its own
evaluation score, so it is an optimistic bound, not a policy.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Positive controls}
The simulator registered an effect of feedback on detection. In synthetic slides, the local-variance policy's recall
margin over this benchmark rose with ROI amplitude, from $-0.045$ at $a_{\mathrm{ROI}}=0$ to $+0.052$ (95\% CI
$+0.033$ to $+0.071$) at $a_{\mathrm{ROI}}=4$ and $+0.101$ ($+0.065$ to $+0.135$) at $a_{\mathrm{ROI}}=8$, positive
on all 8 slides at both amplitudes (Figure~\ref{fig:controls}b). Reconstruction behaved differently: even an oracle
that orders regions by true local difficulty, which no policy could deploy, stayed below the benchmark at every
correlation length, by $0.105$ and $0.066$ PCC at the two
shortest and $0.013$ to $0.020$ at the longer ones (Figure~\ref{fig:controls}a).

The semi-synthetic control failed its criterion. At $a_{\mathrm{spike}}=1$ and $2$, all 20 injected genes entered
the evaluation set on every slide, yet at $a_{\mathrm{spike}}=2$ the residual policy remained below the feasible design, by $0.021$ ($0.012$ to
$0.030$) PCC at 5\% and $0.011$ ($0.004$ to $0.019$) at 10\%, close to its deficits without injection ($0.015$ and
$0.007$; Figure~\ref{fig:main}c, Appendix~\ref{app:spike}). Feedback still helped relative to permuted values, by
$+0.001$ to $+0.013$. The protocol can thus register a large detection effect, but not demonstrably a
reconstruction gain, and the RQ1 results below concern the tested policies.

\subsection{RQ1: Reconstruction}
No feedback policy outperformed the feasible design (Table~\ref{tab:q1abs}, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:q1},
Figure~\ref{fig:main}a). The residual policy fell below it at every budget, by $0.092$ PCC at 0.5\%, $0.057$ at 1\%,
$0.025$ at 2\%, $0.016$ at 5\%, $0.007$ at 10\%, and $0.002$ at 20\%, significantly so at 0.5, 1, 5, and 10\%
($p_H\le0.010$). The local-variance policy was further below, by $0.119$ to $0.163$ (secondary, $p_H\le0.001$).
Bootstrap intervals that repeat the comparator's selection in each replicate excluded zero at every budget (post hoc;
Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}).

The measured values nonetheless carried information. With informative rather than permuted feedback, the residual
policy gained $0.031$, $0.039$, and $0.030$ PCC at 0.5--2\% (secondary, Appendix~\ref{app:secondary}; $p_H$ 0.056, 0.002, 0.014), with no
difference at 10--20\%. Feedback moved the policy toward the feasible design without closing the gap. The
error-reduction design, which shares the residual policy's structure but uses value-independent uncertainty, trailed
the feasible design by similar amounts ($0.107$, $0.067$, and $0.024$ at 0.5--2\%; post hoc), and both converged on
it as the budget grew. The only design to exceed the feasible design was maximum-uncertainty, by $0.003$ PCC at 20\%
(95\% CI $0.002$ to $0.004$, $p=0.0007$, post hoc), a fixed design that uses no feedback. The hindsight benchmark
exceeded the feasible design by $0.016$ to $0.021$ PCC at budgets up to 2\% and by at most $0.002$ above
(Appendix~\ref{app:comparators}).

\subsection{RQ2: Detection}
Informative feedback raised the local-variance policy's recall over permuted feedback by $0.005$, $0.017$, $0.029$,
and $0.063$ at 2, 5, 10, and 20\% (Figure~\ref{fig:main}b, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:q2}). The unadjusted bootstrap
intervals excluded zero at 5--20\%, but intervals from inverting the sign-flip test did so only at 20\%
(Appendix~\ref{app:robust}); no margin survived adjustment ($p_H\ge0.29$), and each estimate lay below its
MDE$_H$ (0.015, 0.035, 0.066, 0.112). The margin over the feasible design was smaller ($0.006$ to $0.026$,
$p_H\ge0.25$), and the margin over the diversity sampler was close to zero ($0.001$ to $0.012$, $p_H=1.000$).

All designs stayed close to random selection, which captures on average the budget fraction of the ROIs
(Table~\ref{tab:q2abs}). At 20\% the local-variance policy exceeded this expectation by $0.078$ recall (95\% CI
from sign-flip inversion $0.029$ to $0.125$) and the feasible design by $0.052$, while at 2\% the feasible design
was slightly below it (0.018 against 0.020). The oracle reached 0.999 at 20\%, so every policy captured only a
small part of the heterogeneity available to it (post hoc; Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

The absolute recalls locate the feedback effect (Table~\ref{tab:q2abs}). At 20\%, the policy recovered 0.278 of the
ROIs with informative feedback but only 0.215 with permuted and 0.204 with constant values, below the feasible
design (0.252); the diversity sampler reached 0.266 without feedback. Part of the feedback effect therefore
recovers ground that the selection rule loses on its own. The margin over constant feedback at 20\% was the only
significant detection effect of feedback in stage 2 ($+0.074$, $p_H=0.038$, secondary).

The apparent advantage also depends on the comparator. Against MI-greedy used on every slide, the local-variance
policy gained $0.043$ and $0.078$ recall at 10 and 20\% ($p_H=0.043$ for both; post hoc). MI-greedy, however, is a
reconstruction design whose detection recall never exceeded that expected of random selection (0.200 at 20\%,
against 0.247 for variance-greedy and 0.252 for the one-shot ranker), so this comparison shows only that feedback
beats chance-level placement. Selection-aware intervals against the feasible design included zero at every budget
(Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}). The ROI-seeking port did not improve on the coverage designs
in stage 1 (Appendix~\ref{app:stage1}); it is not a test of the trained SOFisher, which learned on its own data in a
native run (Appendix~\ref{app:sofisher}). SCR$^2$-ST's processed training data were not public.

\subsection{RQ3: Feedback on top of a fixed design}
No hybrid outperformed its base design (Table~\ref{tab:s3}, Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:s3}). For reconstruction, the
hybrids recovered most of the residual policy's deficit, but the 50\% hybrid remained below $k$-means at
0.5\%, by $0.036$ PCC ($p_H=0.006$), and from 2\% upward every hybrid was within $0.007$ PCC of MI-greedy with no
significant margin. For detection, every hybrid margin lay between $-0.003$ and $+0.006$ recall ($p_H\ge0.76$).
These nulls are tight: at 2--10\% the MDE$_H$ values were 0.004 to 0.017 PCC and 0.004 to 0.026 recall, so gains
above about 0.02 PCC or 0.03 recall would probably have been detected. At 20\% the detection bound widens to
0.035--0.068.

The hybrids responded to the values they were shown. With permuted values they did worse at 2\%, by $0.016$ and
$0.007$ PCC for the two reconstruction hybrids and $0.007$ recall for the 50\% detection hybrid (secondary,

the design. In detection, the local-variance policy run alone (0.137 at 10\%, 0.278 at 20\%) scored above
both hybrids at every budget (descriptive).

\subsection{Stage 4: common evaluation set, tuning, tiles, and headroom}
\emph{Common evaluation set.} Scoring every policy on the same withheld regions left the reconstruction conclusions
unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:s4}). The residual policy stayed below the feasible design at 0.5, 1, and 2\%, by $0.082$,
$0.062$, and $0.032$ PCC ($p_H\le0.012$), no margin above zero was established at any budget, and MSE agreed
(Appendix~\ref{app:stage4}). The 50\% hybrid was within $0.019$ PCC of its base design at every budget, with no
significant margin. The local-variance policy's PCC deficit on the common set was $0.118$ at 2\% against $0.120$ in
stage 2, and $0.093$ against $0.163$ at 20\% (descriptive); the common set changes both the scored regions and the
candidate pool, so we do not attribute the difference to either.

\emph{Value of the measurements.} Against its constant-feedback version, a coverage rule, the local-variance policy
gained $0.010$ to $0.096$ recall, significantly at 20\% ($p_H=0.012$), and the 50\% detection hybrid gained $0.020$
and $0.036$ at 5 and 10\% ($p_H\le0.022$; secondary). For detection, the values therefore added information beyond
coverage. For reconstruction the evidence is weaker than in stage 2. The residual policy's constant-feedback margins are
listed in Appendix~\ref{app:stage4} but not interpreted, because that version selects in index order
(Section~\ref{sec:ablations}), and its margin over permuted feedback fell to $0.017$, $0.011$, and $0.011$ PCC at
0.5--2\%, none significant, against $0.031$ to $0.039$ in stage 2 (descriptive).

\emph{Tuning.} Tuning changed the registered detection conclusion. With its settings chosen for each held-out
study on the other studies, the local-variance policy beat the feasible detection design by $0.016$, $0.023$, and
$0.049$ recall at 2, 5, and 10\% ($p_H=0.001$, $0.013$, $0.013$), and by $0.064$ at 20\% ($p_H=0.072$;
Table~\ref{tab:s4}). Every study received the grid's narrowest kernel, 0.05 of the slide diagonal against the
registered 0.1 (descriptive). Post hoc checks support and qualify the gain (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s4posthoc}). It
held under a bootstrap that repeats both choices in every replicate (2--10\%) and against constant feedback
($+0.012$ to $+0.112$, $p_H\le0.003$; an approximate control, Appendix~\ref{app:stage4}). But with Holm over all
25 stage-4 primary margins only the 2\% margin stays below 0.05, and the diversity sampler, which would have been the
feasible design for every study had it been a candidate, came within $0.009$ to $0.050$ recall ($p_H=0.10$).

\emph{Tiles.} With 300\,$\mu$m tiles, no feedback policy beat the feasible design for reconstruction or detection,
and local-variance in two rounds fell below it at 48 tiles ($-0.059$ recall, $p_H=0.015$;
Appendix~\ref{app:stage4}). Local-variance and the feasible design again captured heterogeneous tiles close to
the random rate (within 0.047 recall of it). These nulls are loose: MDE$_H$ ranged from 0.038 to 0.117 PCC and from
0.036 to 0.083 recall.

\emph{Headroom.} On four slides at 0.5--2\%, the greedy bound exceeded the best fixed design by $0.026$ to $0.166$
PCC, while the residual policy stayed below the fixed design in all 12 cases (Appendix~\ref{app:stage4}). Because the bound
selects on the score it is evaluated on, much of this gap may be optimism, and it does not show that a deployable
policy could do better (descriptive).

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Reconstruction.} Informative feedback beat permuted feedback by up to 0.039 PCC in stage 2 but by at most
0.017, not significantly, on the common set, and neither the policy nor the hybrids improved on a fixed design. Permuted values can mislead a policy, and constant
values reduce the residual policy to index order, so neither contrast measures how much the values were worth for
reconstruction. The pattern fits sensor-placement theory, under which placement matters more than the observed
values when uncertainty depends on locations alone~\citep{krause2008near}. Our imputer's uncertainty has this
property while its predictions do not, so any benefit of feedback had to come through the predictions. The
evaluation does not test the theory, and because neither positive control showed that a reconstruction gain could
be detected and the residual policy was not tuned, it cannot rule out a better feedback policy. What it establishes is that the residual
policy, which targets the imputer's own error and received exact measurements, lost to a fixed design chosen
without the target slide.

\paragraph{Detection.} Feedback beat weak baselines, and once tuned it beat the registered detection designs. It outperformed MI-greedy,
a reconstruction design that performed at chance level for detection (post hoc), and its own constant-feedback
version, a coverage rule, at the largest budget; such gains are what active-search reasoning
predicts~\citep{garnett2012bayesian}, and the synthetic control confirmed that the simulator detects them. With its
registered settings, the policy's margins over a design chosen for detection and over a diversity sampler were
small and not significant, and the diversity sampler's near-parity indicates that heterogeneous regions on these
slides are spread widely enough for broad coverage to find many of them. With a narrower kernel chosen on other
studies, the policy beat the detection design by 0.016 to 0.049 recall at 2--10\% and its own constant-feedback
version by more, so the measured values contributed. The gain is modest: the diversity sampler came within 0.009 to
0.033 recall of the tuned policy at 2--10\%, every design stayed close to random selection, and the oracle shows how
much heterogeneity all of them missed.

\paragraph{Implications.} For developers of adaptive acquisition, the lessons are methodological. Beating random
selection is not evidence that feedback helps, and three comparisons should be standard: the strongest fixed designs available, simple diversity samplers included,
chosen on other data for the same objective, the method itself fed uninformative values that leave its selection rule
working, and the method with settings chosen on held-out data, since our registered settings understated what
local-variance could do. Inference should treat studies, not slides, as the unit. For experimentalists, within
simulated acquisition at 0.5--20\% of regions or 12--48 tiles, one imputer, and computationally defined endpoints, a
fixed design is the practical default for reconstruction: $k$-means at the smallest budgets and MI-greedy above
them. It is computed before any measurement, avoids the turnaround that sequential selection requires, and won
under conditions generous to feedback, since simulated measurements are exact and arrive at once. For capturing
heterogeneous spots, a tuned feedback policy offers 0.02 to 0.05 recall at 2--10\% over the registered detection
designs and 0.01 to 0.03 over a diversity sampler, which has to be weighed against that turnaround; at tile scale it offered no detectable gain. Whether these results hold for other predictors or annotated
biological targets would need prospective or annotated data. They agree with the benchmark's result for predictor
training~\citep{zhu2026benchmarking}, although the two studies address different decisions.

\paragraph{Limitations.} \emph{Simulation.} The simulator is retrospective: a revealed region returns its measured
expression without added noise, batch effects, or delay, conditions that if anything favour feedback. Spots and
128\,$\mu$m HD pseudo-spots are not units any platform selects individually; the 300\,$\mu$m tiles are closer to an
ROI but are still simulated. The batch and block simulations approximate but do not validate any platform.

\emph{Endpoints.} The ROIs are the 20\% of regions with the highest local-variance score, not annotated
structures; high local variance can mark tissue boundaries, mixtures of cell types, or technical noise as well as
targets such as tumour margins or immune niches. The detection results therefore concern computational
heterogeneity and do not show whether feedback helps find a particular biological target. The local-variance
policy estimates from measured values the same kind of statistic that defines the ROIs, and tuning moved its kernel
toward the ROIs' neighbourhood scale, so the detection comparisons are set up favourably for feedback.

\emph{Models and policies.} The 22-slide results use one imputer, whose hyperparameters were set on one of the 22
slides (excluding it changed no conclusion; Appendix~\ref{app:robust}); the $k$-NN and kriging comparisons rest on
five slides. The two feedback policies are hand-built heuristics. Tuning local-variance changed the detection
result, and the residual policy was not tuned, so the reconstruction result bounds what this policy achieves, not
what feedback can achieve. At the tuned kernel width a numerical floor in the policy perturbs its first picks (Appendix~\ref{app:stage4}).
The competitor samplers reimplement objectives; SOFisher was run natively only on its
own data and SCR$^2$-ST not at all.

\emph{Inference.} The registered stage-2 ablation tests use one permutation, with further seeds descriptive.
Multiplicity is controlled within each registered family, not across the four stages or the post
hoc analyses. The plans were registered in our own version-controlled repository rather than with an external
registry, so their timing rests on commit records, which the supplementary material lists; each stage's design was
chosen after earlier results were known, although each was registered before its experiments. The stage-4
constant-feedback contrasts for the residual policy, though registered, are not a valid control. The diversity
sampler was a competitor, not a feasible-design candidate, which favours feedback in the detection comparisons. Budgets above 20\%
were not tested.

\section{Conclusion}

Across 22 human slides from 18 studies and four registered stages, the feedback policies we tested did not earn their
measurement turnaround for reconstruction: the residual policy was significantly worse than a well-chosen fixed design at small budgets,
also when every policy was scored on the same withheld regions, and spending only the remainder of the budget on
feedback did not improve on that design. For capturing heterogeneous regions, the registered policy did not
detectably beat a design built for detection, but with settings chosen on other studies it did, by 0.02 to 0.05
recall at 2--10\%, though not detectably better than a diversity sampler without feedback. The strongest
objective-matched fixed baselines, uninformative-feedback controls, tuning on held-out data,
and study-level inference with positive controls and minimum detectable effects separate the value of feedback from
the value of a good selection rule; new adaptive acquisition methods should be judged against them.

The supplementary material contains the code, configuration, result files, the registered plans with their commit
dates, and scripts that regenerate every table and figure. A public repository will be linked on acceptance; HEST-1k
and UNI2-h are available from their providers.

\section{Configuration and implementation checks}
\label{app:repro}

All values are fixed in the released \texttt{config.py}. The imputer's hyperparameters were set during development
on one prostate slide (TENX174, one of the 22) before any registered analysis and were not changed afterwards; the
policies' hyperparameters were fixed before the stage in which each policy first appears.

Seeds: global 2021; random policy $\{0,\dots,4\}$; the stage-2 permutation uses the global seed, and stage 3 uses
seeds 1 and 2 (1--4 for the repeated local-variance ablation). Imputer: image PCA 64, random Fourier features 512
(median-heuristic bandwidth), ridge 10, graph $k=8$, 20 propagation steps, step size 0.5. $k$-NN imputer: $k=10$,
joint image and space features. Kriging: image PCA 64, ridge 10, residual $k=10$. Placement kernels: feature PCA 64,
median-heuristic lengthscale, noise $10^{-2}$. Error-reduction and residual policies: neighbourhood 15; the residual
policy uses the 256 genes with the highest variance among profiled regions and 10 profiled neighbours.
Local-variance: spatial kernel width 0.1 of the slide diagonal, exploration weight 0.5. Hybrids: coverage fraction
$f\in\{0.5,0.8\}$, rounded up. Batched stage-3 runs: rounds of 5. Spiked control: 20 genes, 3 hotspots, radius 0.05
of the diagonal, noise 0.1 of the reference standard deviation. Statistics: 5{,}000 bootstrap resamples; exact
sign-flip enumeration over studies; MDE from 300 study resamples with 3{,}000 sign flips each, on a grid of 51
effect sizes.

The code for the larger slides replaces dense distance computations with sparse and incremental ones; a 132-run check

the stage-2 run, the five non-adaptive designs reproduced the stage-1 per-slide scores exactly, and the
local-variance and diversity policies reproduced their stage-1 means. A caching error interrupted the first stage-2
run. For the results saved before it, a stale cache entry could only have produced a crash, and the error was fixed

\section{Stage-4 secondary, tile-scale, and headroom results}
\label{app:stage4}

Table~\ref{tab:s4secondary} gives the stage-4 secondary family. The residual policy's and the reconstruction
hybrid's constant-feedback rows compare with a version that selects in index order (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}) and
are not a test of what the values add. Table~\ref{tab:s4tiles} gives the tile-scale families,
Table~\ref{tab:s4u} the ground-truth greedy bound, and Table~\ref{tab:s4posthoc} the post hoc checks of the tuned
policy.

\paragraph{Constant feedback at the tuned kernel width.} Under constant feedback the local-variance term should be
zero everywhere, which would make the constant version identical for every exploration weight and kernel width, so
that the stage-2 constant run would serve as the control for every tuned setting. The policy, however, divides
kernel-weighted sums by the kernel sum clipped below at $10^{-12}$. A candidate more than about 7.4 kernel widths from
every profiled region then receives a spurious variance even under constant values. On three synthetic slides, the
constant-feedback selections were identical across the four exploration weights and the widths 0.1 and 0.2, but
differed at width 0.05 from the second pick onward, the floor acting only in the first few picks. The constant control
in Table~\ref{tab:s4posthoc} therefore uses the width-0.1 run and is approximate for the tuned settings; the exact
control requires running the width-0.05 settings with constant feedback.
