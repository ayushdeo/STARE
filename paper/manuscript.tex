\begin{abstract}
Region-based spatial transcriptomics platforms profile only a subset of a tissue's regions, and the
remaining regions must be reconstructed, usually by predicting expression from co-registered histology.
Which regions to profile is a decision under a budget. We ask whether making this decision adaptively,
by conditioning each next region on the expression measured so far, improves reconstruction relative to
non-adaptive placement rules that fix all regions in advance. We evaluate adaptive acquisition on a
reconstruction simulator built from HEST-1k Visium HD data~\citep{jaume2024hest}, using a frozen UNI2-h
encoder~\citep{chen2024uni} and three imputers: a $k$-nearest-neighbor interpolator, a regression-kriging
model, and a graph-based imputer with nonlinear image features and iterative spatial message passing.
The non-adaptive baselines include variance-greedy and mutual-information-greedy placement from Gaussian-
process sensor-placement theory~\citep{krause2008near} and clustering-based placement. Across seven slides
from four tissues and budgets from 0.5\% to 20\% of regions, we found no statistically significant
improvement of adaptive acquisition over the best non-adaptive baseline at any budget: every paired
margin had a 95\% confidence interval that included or lay below zero. Differences that appeared on a
single slide did not persist across slides. Conditioning the acquisition policy on measured expression
did improve it relative to an image-only variant, but only with the graph-based imputer, and not by
enough to exceed the non-adaptive baselines. In these experiments, reconstruction accuracy depended more
on the imputer and the placement rule than on adaptivity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Whole-transcriptome spatial assays are costly and throughput-limited. Region-of-interest platforms such
as GeoMx DSP~\citep{merritt2020geomx} profile only a small, operator-chosen subset of a tissue's regions.
The unprofiled regions are then reconstructed when needed, typically by predicting expression from
co-registered hematoxylin-and-eosin (H\&E) morphology, a prediction task with a known accuracy
ceiling~\citep{jaume2024hest,xie2023bleep}. How the profiled regions are chosen affects how much of the
transcriptome is recovered at a given budget.

Because each measurement returns real expression values, one might expect a sequential policy that
conditions the next region on the values measured so far (an adaptive, closed-loop policy) to outperform
a policy that fixes all regions in advance. This is the active-feature-acquisition
hypothesis~\citep{yin2020afa}. The region-selection methods currently used in spatial transcriptomics are
non-adaptive: static gene-panel selectors~\citep{covert2023persist,reconst2026} and one-shot region
rankers~\citep{spatialfinder2026}. We test whether adaptivity improves reconstruction, using the
non-adaptive placement rules that Gaussian-process sensor-placement theory identifies as near-optimal for
reconstructing a spatial field: variance-greedy and mutual-information-greedy placement, which are
submodular and therefore admit a $(1-1/e)$ approximation guarantee~\citep{krause2008near}.

We evaluate adaptive acquisition on a reconstruction simulator built from HEST-1k Visium HD data, holding
the image encoder and the imputer fixed across all policies. We sweep budgets from 0.5\% to 20\% of
regions, on seven slides from four tissues, and repeat the comparison under three imputers of increasing
expressiveness. We report the paired difference between adaptive acquisition and the best non-adaptive
baseline on each slide, summarized across slides with a bootstrap confidence interval and a sign test.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We formalize adaptive region acquisition for spatial-transcriptomics reconstruction and pair it with
non-adaptive placement baselines in a reproducible simulator. Across seven slides and three imputers, we
find no statistically significant improvement from adaptivity at any budget. We report a secondary
observation that helps explain this: conditioning the policy on measured expression improves it only when
the imputer's predictions depend on the measured values, which is the condition sensor-placement theory
identifies, but the improvement is not large enough to exceed the non-adaptive baselines. We state the
budgets, tissues, and analysis choices explicitly, and we describe the settings under which adaptivity
might still help.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We summarize the setup here; Section~\ref{sec:methods} gives full detail. A slide is partitioned into
112\,$\mu$m square regions, each equal to one 224\,px UNI2-h patch at 20$\times$~\citep{chen2024uni}. The
inputs are frozen UNI2-h embeddings; the ground truth is log1p-normalized pooled expression. The target is
Pearson correlation (PCC) over the top-50 highly variable genes, scored on the unprofiled regions,
following the HEST-Benchmark protocol~\citep{jaume2024hest}. A policy reveals regions sequentially up to a
budget expressed as a fraction of all regions. A fixed imputer, shared across policies, then reconstructs
the unprofiled regions.

We evaluate three imputers: a $k$-nearest-neighbor interpolator (kNN), a regression-kriging model
(kriging), and a graph-based imputer with nonlinear image features and iterative spatial message passing
(graphflow). We evaluate nine policies: random, farthest-point (grid), $k$-means on image embeddings, a
one-shot region ranker~\citep{spatialfinder2026}, variance-greedy and mutual-information-greedy
placement~\citep{krause2008near}, an adaptive error-reduction policy, an adaptive maximum-uncertainty
policy, and an image-only ablation of the adaptive error-reduction policy. Experiments span seven slides
(prostate $\times 1$, kidney $\times 2$, lung $\times 2$, colon $\times 2$).

We fixed the analysis before the multi-slide run. We defined a positive result as a paired margin,
adaptive acquisition minus the best non-adaptive baseline, with a mean above zero and a 95\% bootstrap
confidence interval excluding zero at at least one budget. On each slide, ``best non-adaptive baseline''
is the highest-scoring non-adaptive policy on that slide.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Comparison with non-adaptive placement}
Table~\ref{tab:absolute} reports absolute reconstruction accuracy. Both the best non-adaptive baseline
and the adaptive error-reduction policy reach positive PCC at every budget; at 20\% budget, for example,
the best non-adaptive baseline reaches 0.707 (kNN), 0.667 (kriging), and 0.684 (graphflow), and the
adaptive policy reaches 0.574, 0.533, and 0.681. Table~\ref{tab:margins} reports the paired margin between
the two, in PCC over the top-50 genes, as the mean and 95\% bootstrap confidence interval over the seven
slides. The margin is the difference between two positive scores, not a score itself. Under the kNN and
kriging imputers, the margin was negative and its confidence interval excluded zero at every budget. Under the graphflow imputer, the margin was smaller and its confidence interval
included zero at the higher budgets: at 10\% budget the margin was $-0.005$ (95\% CI $-0.017$ to
$+0.003$), and at 20\% it was $-0.004$ (95\% CI $-0.009$ to $+0.001$). In no configuration did the margin
meet the pre-specified criterion for a positive result (Figure~\ref{fig:gap}a).

We note the difference between per-slide and cross-slide analysis. On the single prostate slide, the
graphflow adaptive policy exceeded the best non-adaptive baseline by 0.002 to 0.006 PCC at the 5\%, 10\%,
and 20\% budgets. These differences were within the seed-to-seed standard deviation of the random
baseline (about 0.002 to 0.005 PCC at those budgets), and they did not persist when the analysis was
repeated across the seven slides.

\subsection{Non-adaptive baselines}
The non-adaptive baselines were accurate. Averaged over the seven slides under the graphflow imputer,
mutual-information-greedy and $k$-means placement reached PCC of 0.588 and 0.587 at 2\% budget and 0.682
and 0.679 at 20\%. The best non-adaptive baseline on each slide averaged 0.595 at 2\% and 0.684 at 20\%.
The adaptive error-reduction policy reached 0.540 at 2\% and 0.681 at 20\%. The one-shot region
ranker~\citep{spatialfinder2026} was the exception among the non-adaptive baselines, with near-zero PCC at
low budgets, consistent with its objective of covering morphological outliers rather than minimizing
reconstruction error. Because we take the best non-adaptive baseline per slide, this policy is never the
comparison point, so its performance does not affect the result. Our implementation of it is a
reimplementation of the published ranking idea rather than the original system, and its low low-budget
PCC may reflect that.

\subsection{Effect of conditioning on measured expression}
To isolate the contribution of the measured values to the acquisition decision, we compared the adaptive
error-reduction policy with an image-only ablation that uses the same imputer but does not use measured
expression in selection. Under the kNN and kriging imputers, this difference was near zero or negative.
Under the graphflow imputer, it was positive: 0.116 (95\% CI 0.068 to 0.166) at 1\% budget and 0.103
(95\% CI 0.076 to 0.132) at 2\%, positive on all seven slides, decreasing to 0.008 (95\% CI $-0.000$ to
0.019) at 20\% (Figure~\ref{fig:gap}b). The graphflow imputer's predictions depend on the measured
values, through the clamping of profiled regions during message passing, whereas the kNN imputer's
predictive uncertainty depends only on which regions are profiled. The measured values therefore carried
information for the acquisition decision only under the imputer whose predictions depend on them. This
improvement over the image-only ablation was not, however, large enough to exceed the non-adaptive
baselines (Table~\ref{tab:margins}).

\subsection{Effect of imputer choice}
The margin between adaptive acquisition and the best non-adaptive baseline at 20\% budget was $-0.133$
with kNN, $-0.134$ with kriging, and $-0.004$ with graphflow (Table~\ref{tab:margins}). The margin was
closer to zero for the imputer whose predictions depend most strongly on the measured values. At the
budgets and slide count studied here, the margin did not reach a statistically significant positive value
for any imputer.

\subsection{Robustness and scope}
The margins are paired within slide and summarized across seven slides from four tissues. The non-random
policies are deterministic, so they have no within-slide sampling variance; the variation we report is
across slides. The sign split is consistent with the confidence intervals: for example, the adaptive
policy exceeded the best non-adaptive baseline on 0 of 7 slides under graphflow at budgets from 0.5\% to
5\%. Absolute PCC is lower across slides than on the single prostate slide (for graphflow, the best
non-adaptive baseline at 20\% averages 0.684 across slides versus 0.878 on prostate alone), because the
kidney, lung, and colon slides are harder to reconstruct; the pooled estimate is therefore more
conservative than the single-slide estimate.

\section{Relation to sensor-placement theory}
\label{sec:theory}

For a Gaussian process with a fixed, known kernel, the posterior variance at a location depends only on
which locations have been observed, not on the observed values~\citep{krause2008near}. Under that
condition, the variance-minimizing set of measurement locations can be chosen in advance, and observing
the values does not change the optimal choice: adaptive and non-adaptive placement have the same optimum.
The kNN imputer approximates this condition. Its predictive uncertainty is a function of the profiled
region locations and does not use the measured values. This is consistent with the kNN result in
Table~\ref{tab:margins}, where the margin is negative at every budget and becomes more negative as the
budget grows.

When the imputer's predictions depend on the measured values, the adaptive and non-adaptive optima can
differ, and an adaptive policy could in principle improve reconstruction. The kriging and graphflow
imputers have this property, and the ablation in Section~3.3 shows that the graphflow policy does extract
information from the measured values. In our experiments this did not translate into a significant
improvement over the best non-adaptive baseline. One interpretation is that the value of reallocating a
measurement based on observed values was small relative to the value of covering the slide well, which the
non-adaptive placement rules already achieve. This interpretation is consistent with our data but is not
established by them; the experiments test the end-to-end comparison, not this specific decomposition.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Settings where adaptivity might help.}
Adaptivity would be more likely to help when a single measurement changes the imputer's predicted error
substantially and non-locally, for example with imputers whose predictive uncertainty is strongly
heteroscedastic or generative, at very low budgets with strong priors, or in multi-round designs where a
coarse first pass reshapes a second. Each of these predicts a positive margin over the best non-adaptive
baseline with a confidence interval excluding zero, which the present protocol would detect. We did not
observe such a margin here, but we did not test these settings directly.

\paragraph{Practical implication.}
In these experiments, reconstruction accuracy depended mainly on the imputer and on the placement rule.
For budget-constrained reconstruction of the kind studied here, mutual-information or clustering-based
placement computed in advance was as accurate as the adaptive policies we tested, at lower computational
cost.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The graphflow imputer implements several inductive biases associated with flow-matching spatial models
(nonlinear image features, a spatial graph, conditioning on neighboring measured values, and iterative
refinement), but it is not a reproduction of a specific published model, and a fully trained model of that
type might behave differently. We used seven HEST Visium HD slides across four tissues, the tissues used in
prior region-ranking work; the slide count is limited by the availability of Visium HD samples with
whole-transcriptome ground truth. We used the top-50-highly-variable-gene target and patient-stratified
protocol of HEST-Benchmark for comparability with prior histology-to-expression results. The budgets span
low values, where region choice has the largest effect, up to 20\%, where policies converge. We report
uncertainty across slides rather than within slide because the non-random policies are deterministic and
the slide is the unit of generalization. A larger set of slides, additional imputers, or an equivalence
analysis would sharpen these estimates; we report a detection result, not an equivalence result.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Predicting expression from histology.}
Several methods predict spatial expression from H\&E images, including contrastive
retrieval~\citep{xie2023bleep}, flow matching over spatial neighborhoods~\citep{huang2025stflow},
diffusion models~\citep{zhu2025stem}, and autoregressive models~\citep{genar2025}, benchmarked on
HEST-1k~\citep{jaume2024hest}. Reported accuracy is higher for a subset of marker genes and lower for most
genes. We hold the encoder and imputer fixed and study region selection separately.

\paragraph{Selecting what to measure.}
Existing selection methods are non-adaptive. Gene-panel selectors choose a fixed set of
genes~\citep{covert2023persist,reconst2026}; region rankers score regions once by heuristic
diversity~\citep{spatialfinder2026}. These methods do not condition selection on expression measured
during acquisition. We study the adaptive extension and compare it with non-adaptive placement.

\paragraph{Active acquisition and sensor placement.}
Active feature acquisition frames sequential measurement as a policy-learning
problem~\citep{yin2020afa}. Gaussian-process sensor placement provides the non-adaptive baselines used
here and the result that a fixed-kernel posterior makes adaptive and non-adaptive placement
equivalent~\citep{krause2008near}. We apply this comparison to spatial-transcriptomics region acquisition
and report cross-slide confidence intervals.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Data and region grid.}
We use HEST-1k Visium HD samples~\citep{jaume2024hest} from four tissues (prostate, kidney, lung, colon),
seven slides total. Fine bins are pooled into 112\,$\mu$m square regions, each equal to one 224\,px patch
at 20$\times$, the UNI2-h input size, so that region and encoder patch coincide. Per region we compute
log1p-normalized pooled expression. Genes expressed in fewer than 10\% of spots are removed, and the top-50
highly variable genes are selected per slide. The image encoder is UNI2-h (ViT-H, 1536-dimensional),
frozen~\citep{chen2024uni}; a region's embedding is the mean of its patch embeddings, $L_2$-normalized.

\paragraph{Simulator.}
A policy reveals regions one at a time up to a budget. The profiled regions' expression is exposed to the
imputer, which reconstructs the unprofiled regions. All policies use the same imputer and encoder, so the
comparison reflects the selection rule.

\paragraph{Imputers.}
The kNN imputer predicts a region as the mean expression of its 10 nearest profiled regions in a joint
image-and-space feature space; its uncertainty is neighbor variance. The kriging imputer reduces
embeddings to 64 principal components, fits a ridge regression from image to expression on the profiled
regions, and spatially interpolates the residuals; its uncertainty is the residual variance among spatial
neighbors, which depends on the measured values. The graphflow imputer uses a random-Fourier-feature map of
the image principal components (512 features) with ridge regression on the profiled regions, followed by
20 steps of message passing on a fixed 8-nearest-neighbor graph in which profiled regions are held at their
measured values at each step; its uncertainty is one minus the graph influence reaching a region from the
profiled nodes. All hyperparameters are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\paragraph{Policies.}
The non-adaptive policies are random (averaged over 5 seeds), farthest-point (grid), $k$-means on image
embeddings, a one-shot region ranker~\citep{spatialfinder2026}, and variance-greedy and
mutual-information-greedy placement. The placement policies use an RBF kernel on joint image-and-coordinate
features with a median-heuristic lengthscale and an incremental conditional-variance recursion;
mutual-information-greedy uses the ratio $\mathrm{Var}(y\mid A)/\mathrm{Var}(y\mid V\setminus A)$
~\citep{krause2008near}. The adaptive policies are error-reduction (which scores a region by the summed
imputer uncertainty of its neighborhood, recomputed after each reveal) and maximum-uncertainty (which
selects the region of highest imputer uncertainty, recomputed after each reveal). The image-only ablation
is the error-reduction policy with the measured expression withheld from selection.

\paragraph{Analysis.}
The primary metric is per-gene Pearson correlation over the unprofiled regions, averaged over the top-50
genes. For each imputer and budget we record per-slide PCC for every policy. We form paired margins across
the seven slides and summarize each with a percentile bootstrap confidence interval (5000 resamples of the
slides) and an exact two-sided sign test. The pre-specified positive-result criterion, fixed before the
multi-slide run, is a mean margin above zero with a 95\% confidence interval excluding zero at at least
one budget. All reported numbers are regenerated from the result files by the released code.

\section{Conclusion}

We evaluated adaptive, closed-loop region acquisition for spatial-transcriptomics reconstruction against
non-adaptive placement baselines, on seven slides and under three imputers. We found no statistically
significant improvement from adaptivity at any budget. Conditioning the acquisition policy on measured
expression improved it only when the imputer's predictions depended on those values, and not by enough to
exceed the non-adaptive baselines. These results are consistent with the sensor-placement result that a
fixed-kernel posterior makes adaptive and non-adaptive placement equivalent. We report the budgets,
tissues, and analysis choices used, and we identify settings, such as sharply data-dependent imputers and
multi-round designs, under which adaptivity might still improve reconstruction and that our protocol would
detect.
